# Video types and creative intent

Every rule in this skill is applied through the intent card. A deviation is
an error only when it breaks the card. A music video that changes location on
every beat is doing its job; a tutorial that does the same has lost the
viewer. Read the brief, fill the card, show it with the shot list, and judge
the finished video against it, not against a film-school template.

## 1. The intent card

Write it before the shot list. One line per field; leave a field out when
the brief gives no reason to fill it.

| Field | What to write |
|---|---|
| Type | One or more from the table in section 3 (an ad can also be a tutorial) |
| Objective | What the viewer should know, feel or do at the end |
| Audience and platform | Who watches, where, aspect, sound on or off by default |
| Structure | Story, message, workflow steps, argument, or musical sections; in order |
| Style and tone | Look, pace, feeling, references the user gave |
| Entities | The hero and every person, product, object or place that must stay recognisable |
| Must show | Actions, features, steps or facts the video fails without |
| Ending | Payoff, call to action, summary, final reveal, or an intentional open ending |
| Sound | Dialogue, narration, music, ambience, effects; which leads |
| Continuity mode | Strict, flexible or discontinuous (section 2) |

Ask one question only when a missing field changes the video. Otherwise
decide, and state the assumption in one line.

## 2. Continuity mode

| Mode | Use for | Enforce | Allow |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strict | One continuous action or place, dialogue, product handling, a tutorial step that spans shots | Identity, position, who is present, screen direction, object state, light direction, time of day across every cut | Changes the story explains on screen |
| Flexible | Most ads, explainers, documentaries, travel, social stories | Identity of the hero and named entities, light world within a scene, readable geography and who is present inside a scene | New places, time jumps, cutaways between scenes |
| Discontinuous | Music videos, montages, mood pieces, dream sequences, rapid-cut ads | The motifs the card names (palette, a recurring object, the artist's look) and the rhythm | Location, wardrobe, scale and time changing on every cut |

A video can switch mode by section: a strict product demo inside a
discontinuous brand montage. Write the mode per section in the plan.

## 3. Priorities by type

These are priorities, not templates. A video that mixes types takes the
priorities of each part.

| Type | Check first | Relax | A good ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narrative film | Character identity, spatial logic, action continuity, dramatic pacing, setup and payoff | Literal product visibility | The promise of the opening paid off, or a chosen open ending |
| Advertisement | Product visible and recognisable, one clear message, brand look, the call to action | Linear story, physical continuity between unrelated scenes | The product and the next step, readable |
| Product demo | Every claimed feature shown working, hands and product plausible, order of use | Atmosphere shots that do not show the product | The result of using the product |
| Tutorial | Correct step order, each step's result visible, readable interfaces and labels, nothing skipped | Mood, score, shot variety for its own sake | The finished result and a one-line recap |
| Explainer | One idea per scene, the idea introduced before it is used, visuals that literally show the claim | Character arcs | The summary the viewer can repeat |
| Documentary | Factual fidelity, context before detail, illustrative footage that does not mislead | Hook tricks, staged reveals | What is known, said plainly |
| Music video | Cuts on the beat, visual motifs that recur, stylistic coherence, energy that follows the track | Physical and spatial continuity, story logic | The track's last section, visually resolved |
| Social video | The first second, pace, readability on a phone with sound off, platform aspect | Establishing shots, slow builds | A payoff or loop inside the platform's length |
| Animation | Character design held, motion plausible for the style, one animation style throughout | Photographic realism | Per the story or message it carries |
| Presentation | Each point readable and on screen long enough, consistent layout and type | Camera movement, drama | The takeaway, stated |

## 4. Deliberate or broken

Before flagging a deviation, ask the three questions in order:

1. Does the card or the plan call for it (a time jump, a flashback look, a
   music-video wardrobe change, a jump cut on a social cut)?
2. Would the target viewer understand it on first viewing?
3. Does it serve the objective, or does it only happen to be there?

Yes to the first two: it is a choice; leave it. No to the second: it is an
error even if it was intended; say so plainly and offer the smallest fix.

Examples:

- A jump cut inside a 20 s talking-to-camera social clip keeps the pace: a
  choice. The same jump cut inside a continuous dramatic scene reads as a
  missing frame: an error.
- The light turns from warm to cold at the reveal of a product: a choice when
  the plan says the reveal changes the mood. The light turning cold between
  two shots of the same breakfast: an error.
- The tutorial's third step shows a result the second step has not produced
  yet: always an error, whatever the style.

## 5. What not to force

- No hook-and-payoff arc on a tutorial or a presentation; they open on the
  goal and end on the result.
- No establishing shot on a social clip whose first second must be the
  subject.
- No physical continuity on a montage or a music video.
- No music on a video whose card wants silence, ambience or dialogue only.
- No extra shots to reach a length; a shorter video that keeps its promise
  beats a padded one.
