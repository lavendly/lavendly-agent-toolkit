# Lighting and atmosphere

Light shapes the subject, says where and when we are, steers attention and
carries the feeling. Think in visible sources, direction, quality, contrast
and how light meets materials. Write the light into both prompts of every
shot, identically, and keep its direction within a scene.

## 1. The decisions per scene

- Motivated source: what seems to make the light (window, sun, lamp, screen,
  streetlight, fire, the product's own glow, a studio source).
- Direction: front, side, back, top, low, or a named mix. Direction makes
  shape and shadow.
- Quality: soft and broad, hard and defined, or a controlled mix.
- Contrast: how bright the subject is against shadow and background.
- Colour: warm, neutral or cool, from the setting and the intention.
- Separation: rim, background contrast or depth that lifts the subject.
- Falloff: how fast the light fades with distance.
- Surface response: matte, glossy, translucent, metallic, skin, glass,
  liquid, fabric.
- Practicals: lights visible in the scene that motivate the look.
- Highlight protection: texture and labels never clip to white.
- Shadow logic: shadows agree with the source.

## 2. Vocabulary that models follow

key light, fill, backlight or rim, negative fill, bounce, practical light,
motivated light, hard light, soft light, edge light, silhouette, volumetric
light (only with haze, dust or mist that the scene justifies).

## 3. Recipes

### Premium product
- Broad controlled sources for deliberate gradients on glossy surfaces.
- Narrow edge sources to draw glass and metal contours.
- Negative fill to define shape.
- Labels readable, reflections coherent.
- The product stays still; the camera or the light moves.
- Say what the metal reflects; never "random bright streaks".

### Product with its own light (a lamp, a screen, a candle)
- The product is the key light once it is on; the room is lit by it.
- Before it turns on, one cool ambient source (window) from one side.
- The turn-on is the action: warm light blooms, the fall-off across the
  nearest surface is the texture shot.

### Soft window portrait
- Window to one side and slightly in front of the face; broad, soft, gentle
  shadow transition.
- The far side darker but readable; background slightly darker or lit by a
  practical.
- Natural skin texture; never waxy.

### Moody interview or dialogue
- Soft directional key at an angle; restrained fill to keep shape.
- Subject separated by tonal contrast or a subtle edge.
- Eyes visible and dialogue clear over darkness.

### Food
- Side or back-side light for steam, texture and relief; soft fill in the
  shadows.
- Warm practicals for a kitchen; food colour stays believable.
- Overhead shots readable; hand shadows physically consistent.

### Bright studio, clean explainer
- Broad even light; subject separated from a bright background by tone,
  edge or colour.
- No shadows across text or the feature being shown; neutral colour.

### Night street, neon
- Colour motivated by signs, vehicles, windows, street lamps.
- Detail kept in darks, important highlights protected.
- Wet pavement reflects nearby sources consistently; rain only if asked.
- Colour contrast used selectively, not every light saturated.

### Golden hour exterior
- The sun as a directional source, side or back.
- Faces kept by natural fill; shadows and warm highlights agree with the sun.
- One sun position per scene.

### Suspense
- Light restricted to plausible sources and to what must be understood.
- Negative space and controlled shadow conceal on purpose.
- Sound and timing carry the tension; darkness alone is not suspense.

### Interior, architecture
- Window direction and exterior exposure coherent.
- Practicals balanced with daylight without a colour cast.
- Window highlights protected, verticals straight.

## 4. Light and material

- Glass: controlled reflections and edge highlights reveal shape; edges must
  be visible.
- Metal: reflects its environment; name what it reflects.
- Water: reflects and distorts according to surface motion.
- Skin: broad sources, gentle transitions, never plastic.
- Fabric: directional light reveals weave and folds.
- Wood and stone: grazing light reveals texture; too much looks fake.
- Smoke, dust, steam: backlight reveals them; density and direction match
  the room and the heat source.

## 5. Failures to design out

- Shadows from two directions in one frame.
- Rim lights with no source, or too bright.
- Flat light where shape matters; deep shadow over the main action.
- Bloom, flare, haze, floating particles by default.
- Reflections that do not match nearby objects.
- Orange skin, cyan shadows, teal and orange grading as a reflex.
- A background brighter or busier than the subject without a reason.
- Light changing direction between shots of one continuous scene.

## 6. The light line for a prompt

```text
One <soft|hard> <source> from the <direction>, <colour>, the <far side> falling into <shadow depth>; <material> reads as <highlight behaviour>.
```

Example: "One soft window light from the left, cool, the right side falling
into deep shadow; brushed brass reads as a single long highlight."
