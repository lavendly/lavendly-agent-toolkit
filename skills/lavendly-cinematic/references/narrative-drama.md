# Recipe: narrative short film and drama

## 1. Purpose, audience, ending

- Purpose: one emotional story beat that changes a person. Micro-fiction
  for YouTube, festivals, social feeds, a showreel.
- Audience: adults who reward restraint and notice detail.
- The ending must show the change: the new state held on screen (the phone
  buried, the train not taken), or an open ending the film chose and framed
  as a question.

## 2. Structures by length

The shot lists are worked examples (a boy on a platform; Marisol, a woman
in her forties, burying a phone in her back garden at dusk). The user's
story replaces them and keeps the shape.

Model lengths are for Veo 3.1 Fast (4 or 8 s; 6 s costs the same as 8 s).
On Kling or Seedance, generate 5 s for a cut up to 5 s and 10 s up to 10 s.

### 90 s: three acts with a midpoint

The 60 s list with five rows added at the midpoint (after row 7), where the
power shifts: 7a wide, the back door opens and a man stands in the light
(introduced here, before he acts, with his locked description); 7b medium
close-up, he looks at her hands; 7c over the shoulder from behind him, she
stands; 7d close-up, she holds his look; 7e insert, his hand closes the door
halfway. Seventeen shots; the climax moves to about 75 s.

## 3. Hook and payoff

- Hook (0 to 2 s): the middle of an action or a face mid-feeling that asks a
  question (why is she burying a phone). No titles before 3 s.
- Payoff: the change shown, then held: the longest shot in the film, static
  or a slow pull-back, 3 to 6 s, ambience only or the score's last chord.

## 4. Camera, light, colour

- Camera: mostly locked-off; slow push-ins on realisation; handheld only for
  crisis, with subtle sway; 35mm for scenes, 85mm for faces, 100mm inserts.
  Keep the 180 degree line in every dialogue.
- Light: motivated practicals (a pendant lamp, a window, a street lamp) with
  one colour contrast, warm interior against cool exterior. Low key for
  tension, soft side light for intimacy.
- Colour: restrained; a muted earth palette; lifted blacks for memory;
  desaturated for grief.

Exact phrases that work:

- "Narrative short film still, 35mm film look, fine grain"
- "lit only by the warm pendant lamp above the table, the window behind her deep blue"
- "soft north-facing window light from camera left, the right side of her face in shadow"
- "slow dolly push-in toward her face over the whole shot, stopping at a close-up"
- "handheld camera with subtle natural sway, staying on her"
- "Palette: amber, olive, navy"

## 5. Sound

- Native ambience on every shot (room tone, wind, rain, a distant train):
  `native` with no narration, `mix` at 0.3 under a narrator.
- Silence before the turn; the score enters late and quietly.
- Narration only when the film is told by someone (a letter read aloud, a
  memory): 2.3 words per second of the shots it covers; 30 s holds about 65
  words, but most dramas use fewer than 20.
- Dialogue: lines of 3 to 10 words; 15 s holds about 2 lines; 60 s holds 6
  to 10 lines in total ([dialogue-scenes.md](dialogue-scenes.md)).
- Score brief (one for the film): "Sparse felt piano and a low cello. Silent
  for the first third. A single held cello note enters on the choice, a
  three-note piano figure on the climax, resolving to one soft chord on the
  last image. About 70 BPM, instrumental, never busy." Volume 0.3 to 0.4.

| Shot type | Mix mode |
|---|---|
| Dialogue, sound-led action | `native` |
| Ambience under narration | `mix`, volume 0.3 |
| Montage over the score only | `off` |

## 7. Prompt pairs

### Pair 1: the platform (Veo 3.1 Fast, 15 s shot 4)

Still frame:

```text
Narrative short film still, 35mm film look, fine grain. Wide shot, low angle, 35mm lens. An empty rural train platform at blue hour. Noah: a boy of about sixteen, thin, pale, short dark hair, an oversized navy parka with a torn left pocket, a black duffel bag. He stands at the yellow edge line facing the tracks. Sodium-vapour lamps glowing orange above, mist over the fields. Palette: navy, orange, grey.
```

Motion:

```text
Narrative short film, 35mm film look, one continuous take. Locked-off camera. 0 to 3 s: Noah stands still at the edge line; mist drifts across the fields. 3 to 6 s: a train headlight appears far down the tracks and grows in the mist. 6 to 8 s: he turns his head toward the light, then back to the empty platform, and does not move. Noah, the platform and the lamps stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one person in frame, nobody else on the platform. SFX: a distant train horn, rails humming. Ambient noise: wind, a crow. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

### Pair 2: the burial (Veo 3.1 Fast, 30 s shot 6)

Still frame:

```text
Narrative short film still, 35mm film look, fine grain. Close-up at a high angle, 85mm lens, shallow depth of field. Marisol's hands, a woman in her forties: short unpainted nails, a thin silver ring on the right middle finger, the cuffs of a rust-red wool sweater. Both hands rest flat on fresh dark soil in a garden bed at dusk, a faint glow under the soil between them. Cool dusk light from above, a warm window glow from the right edge. Palette: rust, soil brown, slate blue.
```

Motion:

```text
Narrative short film, 35mm film look, one continuous take. Very slow push-in toward the hands over the whole shot. 0 to 3 s: the faint glow under the soil pulses once. 3 to 6 s: her hands press down slowly and smooth the soil flat, fingers spread. 6 to 8 s: the glow is gone; her hands stay still on the soil. Her hands, her ring, her sleeves and the garden light stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly two hands in frame, nobody else. Sound: soil pressed under palms, a far dog bark. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

### Pair 3: the window (Kling v3 Pro, 30 s shot 3)

Still frame:

```text
Narrative short film still, 35mm film look. Medium close-up, eye level, 85mm lens. Marisol: a woman in her forties, olive skin, dark hair tied back loosely, a small mole under her right eye, a rust-red wool sweater. She kneels in a garden at dusk, looking up and to screen right. Warm window glow falls on her face from the right, cool dusk sky behind. Palette: rust, slate blue, warm yellow.
```

Motion:

```text
Scene: a back garden at dusk. Character: Marisol kneeling, looking up at a lit window off screen right. Action: she holds the look; her jaw sets; at 3 s she breathes out slowly through her nose and lowers her eyes to the soil in front of her. Camera: static, eye level. Audio: wind in the hedge, a television murmur from the house. Marisol, her sweater and the light stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one person in frame, nobody else. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles.
```

Second family notes: on Seedance write the same beat in 30 to 100 words,
subject first, and add "already in motion from the first frame" because
Seedance spends its motion in the first two seconds.

## 8. Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| The face drifts between shots | Locked description verbatim; reference wired; app stills for the hero close-ups ([dialogue-scenes.md](dialogue-scenes.md) section 7) |
| Over-acting (tears at second 0, a grimace) | Behaviour words, the change timed late, "holds still for two seconds" ([acting.md](acting.md)) |
| Props and wardrobe change across cuts | The wardrobe line verbatim; the prop's state written in "from" and "to" of the plan |
| The ending gesture nobody asked for | Write the end state and the hold: "her hands stay still on the soil" |
| A shot adds nothing | Cut it from the plan before paying; a shorter film that keeps its promise wins |
| Every shot the same size | Change size between neighbours; every third shot an insert or a reaction |
| A phone or letter shows gibberish text | Keep screens and pages turned away or glowing blank: "the screen faces away from camera" |
| The film reads as a slideshow of pretty frames | Each shot has an event (something changes), not a state |

## 9. Policy guards

- Fictional people only; no real person's name, face or voice.
- No director or studio names for a look; write the lens, light and palette.
- Self-harm, abuse and grief handled with care: implied, never shown, and
  never instructional.
