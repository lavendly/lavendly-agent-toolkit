# Story structures

A short film is a promise and its delivery. The arc is chosen before the
shot list, from the menu below, by what the ending must do. Beats are units
of change (something new is known, done or felt); shots are how a beat is
seen. A beat can take one shot or two.

## 1. Budgets by length

Narration at about 2.3 words per second of the shots it covers (the
platform's ceiling is 2.5; a voice longer than its picture freezes the last
frame). Dialogue inside a generated clip at about 1.2 words per second for
clean sync, never over 2.

| Length | Beats | Shots | Narration words | Dialogue words in clips | Longest hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 s | 2 | 1 to 2 | up to 12 | up to 7 | the last 2 s |
| 15 s | 3 to 4 | 3 to 4 | up to 32 | up to 16 | 3 s on the turn |
| 30 s | 6 to 8 | 6 to 8 | 65 to 70, never over 75 | 25 to 30 | 3 to 4 s on the payoff |
| 60 s | 10 to 12 | 10 to 14 | 130 to 140, never over 150 | 50 to 60 | 4 s on the payoff |
| 90 s | 15 to 17 | 15 to 20 | 190 to 205, never over 225 | 75 to 90 | 4 to 5 s on the payoff |

Proportions that hold at every length: the opening hook in the first 2 s,
setup 15 to 25 percent, development 50 to 60 percent, the turn and payoff
15 to 20 percent, a held last image of 2 to 4 s.

## 2. The arc menu

Each arc lists its beats in order, the beat count per length, the genres it
serves and the camera rhythm that suits it. Adjacent beats never share a
camera move; the payoff beat is static.

### 2.1 Three acts in miniature

Beats: world and want; the inciting moment; first attempt; complication;
low point; the choice; climax; new state.

| Length | Beats used |
|---|---|
| 15 s | world and want, inciting moment, climax as a held reaction |
| 30 s | world and want, inciting moment, attempt, complication, choice, climax, new state (7) |
| 60 s | all eight, with two attempts and two complications (11) |
| 90 s | all eight, with a midpoint reversal between the attempts (16) |

Genres: drama, sci-fi, period, romance. Camera rhythm: static wide, push-in,
static medium, handheld or tracking on the complication, push-in on the
choice, static on the climax, pull-back on the new state.

### 2.2 Cold open, then the story

Beats: a striking moment from the middle (or the end) of the story; cut to
earlier; the story builds back toward that moment; the moment again, now
understood; one step beyond it.

| Length | Beats |
|---|---|
| 15 s | the moment, earlier, the moment understood (3) |
| 30 s | the moment, earlier (3 beats), the moment, one step beyond (6) |
| 60 s | the moment, earlier (7 beats), the moment, two steps beyond (11) |

Genres: thriller, noir, drama, trailers. Rule: the cold open shot is
generated once and its second appearance is a different angle or a longer
take of the same action, never the same clip twice. Mark the time jump with
light, a dip to black (`fade_out_ms`, `fade_in_ms`), or a line of narration.

### 2.3 Man in a hole

Beats: a normal day; trouble falls; it gets worse; the bottom; the way out;
better than before.

| Length | Beats |
|---|---|
| 30 s | 6 |
| 60 s | 10 (two "worse" beats, two "way out" attempts) |
| 90 s | 15 |

Genres: drama, comedy, action. Camera rhythm: eye level steady at the start;
high angle at the bottom; low angle on the way out.

### 2.4 Story spine

Once there was; every day; until one day; because of that; because of that;
until finally; ever since then. Seven beats; a natural 30 s at about 4 s per
beat, or 60 s with each "because of that" doubled.

Genres: fables told as live action, period, gentle drama. Works with a
narrator: one sentence per beat.

### 2.5 Tension ladder

Beats: calm; a wrong detail; a sound; a second wrong detail, bigger; false
calm; the real scare or reveal; the sting; black.

| Length | Beats |
|---|---|
| 6 s | normal image, the wrong detail, cut (2) |
| 15 s | calm, unease, a sound, the reveal, black (4 to 5) |
| 30 s | calm, three rungs of wrongness, false calm, scare, sting (7) |
| 60 s | two false scares and one real one (11 to 12) |
| 90 s | three rungs, a false resolution, two real scares, a last image that reopens it (16) |

Genres: horror, thriller. Camera rhythm: long locked-off holds, slow push-ins
that never arrive, then two to three very short shots at the scare.

### 2.6 Rule of three

Beats: premise; first instance; second instance, bigger; third instance
breaks the pattern (the punchline); reaction; tag.

| Length | Beats |
|---|---|
| 6 s | setup, punchline (2) |
| 15 s | setup, escalation, punchline, reaction button (4) |
| 30 s | premise, two escalations, twist, button (6) |
| 60 s | premise, three escalations, reversal, tag, a final tag (10) |

Genres: comedy. Camera rhythm: the same framing for all three instances (the
repetition is the joke), a different size only on the punchline.

### 2.7 Mystery: question, clues, answer

Beats: the question (a body, a missing object, a stranger); first clue;
false lead; second clue; the connection; the answer shown; what it costs.

| Length | Beats |
|---|---|
| 30 s | question, clue, false lead, clue, answer, cost (6) |
| 60 s | question, three clues, two false leads, connection, answer, cost (10) |
| 90 s | as 60 s plus a second question raised by the answer (15) |

Genres: noir, mystery, thriller. Every clue gets an insert of 3 to 4 s the
viewer can read; the answer refers back to one of them.

### 2.8 Twist or reversal

Beats: an apparent situation; details consistent with it; one detail that
fits both readings; the reveal; the opening seen again in the new reading.

| Length | Beats |
|---|---|
| 15 s | situation, ambiguous detail, reveal (3) |
| 30 s | situation, two consistent details, the double detail, reveal, re-read (6) |
| 60 s | as 30 s with a false reveal before the true one (10 to 11) |

Rules: fair play (every clue was on screen); the reveal is a picture, never a
line of dialogue alone; the last shot echoes the first in framing so the
viewer re-reads the film.

### 2.9 Chase or ticking clock

Beats: the goal and the deadline; the start; obstacle one; obstacle two,
closer; the near miss; the last obstacle; arrival or failure; the breath
after.

| Length | Beats |
|---|---|
| 15 s | the goal, one obstacle, arrival (3 to 4) |
| 30 s | 7 |
| 60 s | 11 to 12 |

Genres: action, thriller. Cutting accelerates toward the near miss (shots of
2 to 3 s), then one held shot of 4 s for the breath.

### 2.10 Meet, test, choose

Beats: two people in one place; a look; a small risk taken; a test (a
misunderstanding, a cost); the choice; the held moment.

| Length | Beats |
|---|---|
| 15 s | the look, the risk, the held moment (3) |
| 30 s | 6 |
| 60 s | 10 (two tests) |

Genres: romance, drama. Camera rhythm: singles that grow closer, a two-shot
only at the choice.

### 2.11 Vignette

Beats: one image held; one change in it; the held result. A 6 or 15 s film.
Genres: drama, horror, sci-fi teasers. The change must be visible in a muted
viewing.

### 2.12 Circle

Beats: an opening image; the journey; the same image, changed by the journey.
Any length; the first and last still prompts share framing and place, and
differ in one named detail (the cup is now empty, the chair is now taken).

### 2.13 Trailer shape

Setup, complication, escalation montage, the drop, title room, button. The
beat counts and timings are in [trailers-teasers.md](trailers-teasers.md).

## 3. Setup and payoff

- Every object, line or skill the ending relies on is shown earlier, on its
  own, long enough to register (an insert of 3 to 4 s, or a line).
- Plant early (first third), remind once in the middle if the film is over
  30 s, pay off in the last quarter.
- The payoff is shown, not described: the ring in her palm, not "she still
  has the ring".
- Every setup pays off. A planted gun that never fires reads as a mistake.
- Write the pairs into the shot list ("shot 2 plants the key; shot 7 pays it
  off") and check both landed frames before the render.

## 4. Three acts in miniature, timed

| Act | 30 s | 60 s | 90 s | What it must do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0 to 2 s | 0 to 3 s | 0 to 4 s | A face, an action or a wrong detail that asks a question |
| Setup | 2 to 8 s | 3 to 12 s | 4 to 18 s | Who, where, what they want |
| Inciting moment | 8 to 11 s | 12 to 16 s | 18 to 24 s | The thing that will not let them stay |
| Development | 11 to 23 s | 16 to 45 s | 24 to 70 s | Attempts and complications; at 90 s a midpoint reversal at 45 s |
| Climax | 23 to 27 s | 45 to 55 s | 70 to 84 s | The choice, then the act |
| Resolution | 27 to 30 s | 55 to 60 s | 84 to 90 s | The new state, held |

## 5. The cold open

- Frame 0 is a face mid-feeling, a hand mid-action, or a wrong detail. Never
  a fade-up from black, never an establishing landscape with nothing in it.
- Start inside the action: the clip's motion prompt says "already in motion
  from the first frame".
- The question it raises is answered on screen before the end.
- In 9:16 the face sits between 15 and 55 percent of frame height.

## 6. The twist

- Decide the twist first and write the film backward from it.
- Show the clue that makes it fair in a shot of its own; the viewer may
  ignore it the first time.
- The reveal shot is static and held 3 s or more; the reaction comes after.
- No twist in a 6 s film: there is room only for a turn.

## 7. Endings menu

| Ending | When | Last shot |
|---|---|---|
| Closed | Drama, romance, comedy | The new state, held 3 s, static |
| Open by choice | Drama, horror | A question visibly asked: a door left ajar, a phone ringing unanswered |
| Reversal | Thriller, twist | The reveal, then the reaction |
| Circle | Drama, mystery | The opening framing with one detail changed |
| Button | Comedy, trailer | A short extra beat after the punchline or title: a look, a sound |
| Sting | Horror | Two frames of the threat, then black, then one sound |

The last shot is an action by the hero or a held image the plan names. Never
end on a gesture the prompt did not ask for: write the end state.

## 8. Two shots per beat under narration

When a narrator carries the beat (noir, a letter read aloud, a fable), give a
beat of 8 to 10 s of narration two shots: a wide that places the line, then a
detail cut in mid-sentence. The voice runs continuously across the cut. This
is the single biggest rhythm gain in narrated films.
