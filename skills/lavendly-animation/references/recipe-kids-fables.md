# Recipe: kids' fables and bedtime stories

Gentle stories with a small lesson for children of 3 to 8 and the adults
who watch with them. Any look works (watercolour, felt, soft 3D, paper);
the rules here are about story, pace, sound, narration and safety, and they
apply on top of the style recipe.

## 1. Purpose, audience, ending

- Fable: a small problem, tries, help, a lesson shown by what the hero
  does. The ending shows the lesson in action; the narrator says it in one
  plain line at most, never as a sermon.
- Bedtime story: the energy falls from the first shot to the last. The
  ending is the hero falling asleep, the light dimmed to one warm source,
  the camera still, the narrator's last line soft and slow.

## 3. Hook and payoff

- Hook (0 to 2 s): a cute hero doing something curious, and a question in
  the narration ("What is Pebble listening for?").
- Payoff: the hero's own action solves it, the lesson shows in that action,
  and the last shot holds still for 3 to 4 s.
- Bedtime: no hook jolt. Open on a calm, warm image; the question is soft.

## 4. Camera, light, colour

- Camera: slow and gentle: locked-off frames, soft push-ins, slow pans. No
  shaky camera, no fast cuts, no extreme low angles that make anything loom.
- Pace: average shot 4 to 6 s; the last shots longer than the first.
- Framing: medium shots for acting; avoid open-mouth close-ups; every third
  shot an insert or a view from above (paws, the acorn, the moon).
- Light: warm soft daylight, golden hour, lantern or night-light glow at
  night with a soft blue moonlight fill. Never frightening low key: shadows
  stay soft and readable.
- Colour: warm pastels or a warm saturated storybook palette; night scenes
  in soft indigo and lavender, never black.
- Bedtime colour script: the light warms and dims across the film until one
  warm source remains.

## 5. Sound and narration

- Native: soft SFX only (a chirp, a pop, a rustle, a gentle chime); `mix` at
  0.3 under narration. No sudden loud sounds; nothing louder than the voice.
- Narration for children: 1.8 to 2.1 words per second; 15 s about 28
  words, 30 s about 55, 60 s about 100. Short sentences, one image per
  sentence, present tense, simple words, a repeated refrain ("Not yet, said
  Pebble."), feelings named plainly ("Pebble feels scared of the dark."),
  numbers and sounds a child can join in with.
- The narrator: one warm, calm voice for the whole film. Play two candidates
  on two real lines and let the user pick by ear (ElevenLabs names such as
  george, matilda or lily are starting points on the OpenRouter voice route;
  with direct ElevenLabs pass a premade id; the list and the rule are in
  [mcp-calls.md](mcp-calls.md) section 7). For bedtime, the last line can carry the v3 delivery tag
  "[whispers]".
- Character voices: rare; when a character speaks inside a clip, one short
  line, medium close-up, mouth described gently; otherwise the narrator
  quotes them.
- Score brief: "Ukulele, glockenspiel and soft strings at about 84 BPM,
  gentle and warm; the glockenspiel answers the firefly's appearance; for
  bedtime it slows across the film and ends on a single soft music-box
  phrase that fades." Volume 0.2 to 0.3 with `ducking: true`; the score's
  node gets `fade_out_ms`, since a fade on the last clip does not fade it.
- Captions: for the adult reading along; a large, calm style (`youtube` or
  `caption`), at most about 17 characters per second.

## 7. Prompt pairs (Veo dialect)

Pair 1: the night-light goes out (shots 1 and 2 of the 15 s cut played as
one 6 s take, generated at 8 s)

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Needle-felted stop-motion animation, visible wool fibres and stitching, miniature fabric set. Pebble: a small round brown bear cub on four legs, cream muzzle, round black bead eyes, small round ears, a green knitted nightcap. Pebble lies in a moss bed in a cosy cave bedroom, blanket to his chin, a glowing acorn night-light on a stone beside him. Wide shot, eye level. Warm acorn glow from the right, soft blue moonlight from the cave mouth. Palette: honey, moss green, indigo, cream. No text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Needle-felted stop-motion animation, one continuous take, stepped at 12 frames per second. 0 to 2 s: the acorn night-light flickers twice. 2 to 3 s: it goes dark; the room falls into soft blue moonlight. 3 to 6 s: Pebble's eyes open wide; he pulls the blanket up to his nose and holds still. Camera: locked-off. End state: Pebble awake under his blanket, the acorn dark. The cave and the moonlight stay as in the opening frame; shadows stay soft. Exactly one bear cub, nobody else. SFX: a tiny fizzle, a soft blanket rustle. Ambient noise: quiet night crickets. No music. Clean frame: no text, no on-screen graphics.
```

Pair 2: the firefly helps (the payoff)

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Needle-felted stop-motion animation, visible wool fibres and stitching, miniature fabric set. Pebble: a small round brown bear cub on four legs, cream muzzle, round black bead eyes, small round ears, a green knitted nightcap. Pebble lies in his moss bed, the dark acorn on the stone beside him, one small firefly with a soft yellow glow hovering at the cave mouth. Medium shot, eye level. Soft blue moonlight, the firefly's glow. Palette: honey, moss green, indigo, cream. No text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Needle-felted stop-motion animation, one continuous take, stepped at 12 frames per second. 0 to 3 s: the one firefly drifts across the room and settles inside the acorn shell. 3 to 5 s: the acorn glows warm again and lights Pebble's face. 5 to 8 s: Pebble smiles, mouth closed, his eyes close slowly, and he holds still, asleep. Camera: slow push-in, stopping on his face. End state: Pebble asleep beside the glowing acorn. Exactly one bear cub and one firefly. SFX: a soft glockenspiel chime as the acorn lights. Ambient noise: quiet crickets. No music. Clean frame: no text, no on-screen graphics.
```

Second family (Wan 2.7): subject, scene, motion, aesthetic, then "felt
style": "Pebble the felt bear cub in his moss bed; the firefly settles in
the acorn, it glows, he smiles and falls asleep. Slow push-in. Soft blue
moonlight and warm glow, low contrast. Felt style, stepped stop-motion.
Sound: a soft chime, crickets. Clean frame: no text, no on-screen graphics."

## 8. Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Scary artefacts: extra eyes, teeth, a stretched jaw | medium shots, mouth closed by default, QC every frame before the next scene |
| Two of the hero, or an extra creature | count line in every prompt; a newcomer (the firefly) gets its own introduction shot |
| The cub stands up and walks like a person | "on four legs" in every still; "on all four paws" in every walking shot |
| Colour drifts warm or green across chained scenes | start each scene from a fresh still written from the bible after 2 hops |
| An ending gesture nobody asked for | the last beat is one named action and a hold |
| Darkness turns frightening | name the soft fill ("soft blue moonlight"), "shadows stay soft" |
| Narration too fast for children | count words at 2.0 per second; cut words, never speed the voice |

## 9. Safety and policy guards

- Nothing frightening: no sharp teeth, looming shadows, chases, monsters or
  peril that is not resolved in the same scene. Mild worry, quickly eased.
- Nothing a child could copy and get hurt by: no climbing on high shelves,
  playing with fire or candles, eating berries or mushrooms found outside,
  wandering off alone without a grown-up character in the story.
- No flashing light faster than three flashes in any one second; no strobe.
- No product placement or calls to buy aimed at children.
- No real children's faces or likenesses, no existing characters, no
  studio or book names.
- The film is AI-made: say so if the user asks, and remind the user that
  the platform's "made for kids" setting is theirs to choose when posting.
