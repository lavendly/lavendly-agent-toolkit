# Recipe: 2D cartoon

Two families: the modern TV cartoon (thick outlines, flat fills, simple
painted backgrounds) and the 1930s rubber-hose look (black and white, bendy
limbs, white gloves). Both described by craft, never by studio or character.

## 1. Purpose, audience, ending

Gags, sketches, explainers with a character, channel bumpers, kids' and
all-ages social clips. The ending is a held reaction after the gag (the
dazed sit, the eye take, the shrug to camera) or the button: a tiny second
gag that tops the first.

## 3. Hook and payoff

- Hook (0 to 2 s): the character already mid-action with a strong silhouette
  (a stretch, a sneak, a pounce) or a clear want in one frame (a character
  and the one thing it wants).
- Payoff: the reversal lands fast (under 1 s) and the reaction holds 1 to
  1.5 s. The button, when there is one, is shorter than the main gag.

## 4. Camera, light, colour

- Staging: flat and theatrical. Side views and straight-on frames, the
  character in clear silhouette against a simple background.
- Moves: locked-off for gags, trucking along the background for chases, a
  cut to a close-up for an eye take (a crash zoom can render as a jump
  inside the shot; a cut is safer).
- Cartoon physics in words: "hangs in the air for a beat before falling",
  "a smear of motion as he zips off", "his eyes pop wide", "a dust puff
  where he stood".
- Light: flat and even, one hard shadow tone at most; time of day by colour
  of the background, not by light effects.
- Colour: 4 to 5 flat colours plus black line; strong complementary pairs
  (purple and mustard, red and teal).
- Exact phrases, modern: "2D cartoon animation, thick even black outlines,
  flat colour fills, simple painted backgrounds", "snappy cartoon timing, a
  quick anticipation, a fast move, a held pose", "flat colours, thick
  outlines, the drawing stays flat". Retro: "black and white 2D cartoon,
  rubber-hose limbs, white gloves, light film grain and gentle scratches",
  "bouncy looped motion on twos".

## 5. Sound

- Native: cartoon SFX carry the gag: boing, slide whistle, xylophone run,
  pop, a dust whoosh, a sneeze. Gag clips `native` at 1.
- Narration: rare; a deadpan narrator line can set up a sketch: 15 s up to
  25 words, 30 s up to 50.
- Dialogue: one short exclamation per clip at most, written with a full
  stop (`Mo shouts: "Hey."`). Prefer wordless gags.
- Score brief: "Bouncy brass, xylophone and pizzicato strings at about 128
  BPM, playful and light; it stops dead for the beat before the leaf falls,
  then one bright brass hit on the sneeze and a short tag." Retro: "Tuba,
  banjo and muted trumpet at about 120 BPM, two-beat bounce."
- Mix: gag clips `native` at 1, score 0.25 to 0.3 with `ducking: true`; a
  music-led bumper sets clips to `mix` at 0.5 and the score at 0.6.

## 7. Prompt pairs (Veo dialect)

Pair 1: the sneeze (the 6 s gag)

Still-frame prompt:

```text
2D cartoon animation, thick even black outlines, flat colour fills, simple painted backgrounds. Mo: a small round purple moose on four legs, tiny stubby antlers, a big pink nose, large white oval eyes with black pupils, a green striped scarf. Mo stands on a grassy hill under a bare tree, looking up at the one red leaf left on a branch. Wide shot, eye level, side view. Flat even afternoon light. Palette: purple, leaf red, mustard, sky blue. No text, no letters.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
2D cartoon animation, one continuous take. Snappy cartoon timing: a quick anticipation, a fast move, a held pose. 0 to 2 s: the one red leaf wobbles and lets go; Mo's eyes follow it down. 2 to 4 s: it lands on the tip of his nose and his eyes cross to look at it. 4 to 6 s: he scrunches up, sneezes so hard his body squashes and springs backward, and he lands sitting on all four legs splayed, dazed, the leaf gone. Camera: locked-off, side view. End state: Mo sitting dazed under the tree. Flat colours, thick outlines, the drawing stays flat. Exactly one Mo and one leaf, nobody else. SFX: a soft flutter, a slide whistle down, a big sneeze, a boing. No music. Clean frame: no text, no on-screen graphics.
```

Pair 2: the rubber-hose bounce (a retro bumper)

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Black and white 2D cartoon, rubber-hose limbs, white gloves, light film grain and gentle scratches. Bertie: an old round tea kettle with a curly spout for a nose, pie-cut eyes, thin black rubber-hose arms and legs, white four-finger gloves, white shoes. Bertie stands on a stove top beside one cup and saucer, knees bent, arms out. Medium shot, eye level, straight-on staging. Flat even light, greys from white to deep black. No text, no letters.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Black and white 2D cartoon, one continuous take. Bouncy looped motion on twos. 0 to 4 s: Bertie bounces in place on a steady beat, knees and arms swinging in loose curves, a puff of steam from the spout on every bounce. 4 to 6 s: he whistles one long note, lifts his lid like a hat toward camera and holds the pose. Camera: locked-off. End state: Bertie holding his lid up, smiling. The stove, the cup and the grey tones stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one kettle character and one cup, nobody else. SFX: springy boings on each bounce, a kettle whistle. Clean frame: no text, no on-screen graphics.
```

Second family (Wan 2.7), pair 1: subject, scene, motion, then the style
word: "Mo, the small purple moose with the green scarf, under a bare tree on
a hill. The one red leaf falls onto his nose; he sneezes, springs backward
and lands sitting, dazed. Locked-off side view. Flat even light, purple and
mustard palette. 2D cartoon, thick outlines, flat colour. Sound: slide
whistle, sneeze, boing. Clean frame: no text, no on-screen graphics."

## 8. Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Shading gradients creep in (looks like 3D) | "flat colour fills, the drawing stays flat"; the plate as reference |
| Outline thickness changes shot to shot | "thick even black outlines" verbatim; similar camera distance between neighbours |
| Off-model in extreme poses | one extreme pose per clip; the hero block in every still |
| Smear frames become melted noise | ask for the smear once, on a fast exit, under 1 s |
| Several gags in one clip | one gag per clip; the button gets its own shot |
| Cartoon physics read as an error | name the convention: "hangs in the air for a beat before falling" |
| Speech bubbles or sound words drawn in frame | clean-frame line; never write sound words as visual elements |
| Four-legged character stands up between shots | "on four legs" in every still and motion prompt |

## 9. Policy guards

- No studio or character names, and no design that echoes a famous cartoon
  (a mouse with round black ears and red shorts, a grey rabbit with white
  gloves, a duck in a sailor shirt, a yellow sponge): change species, shape
  and outfit.
- Retro looks are described by technique, never by a named film or series.
- Slapstick stays painless for kids' audiences: characters are dazed, never
  hurt, and recover in the same scene.
