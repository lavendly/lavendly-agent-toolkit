# Creatures: anatomy, gait, legs, mouths, eyes

Animal and creature heroes fail in ways people do not: a fox that stands up
and runs on two legs, a fifth leg during a turn, a beak that grows teeth, two
eyes looking two ways. Children notice all of it. Write the anatomy into the
prompts; never leave it to the model.

## 1. The creature sheet

Built before any clip, on a plain light grey background, even soft light, one
creature per sheet:

- Full body: side view, three-quarter front, front, back, standing on all
  feet as the species stands.
- The head as its own close-up, cropped out of the body panels: this is the
  single source of the face (several small faces get averaged into a generic
  one).
- Mouth open and mouth closed close-ups, so speech and yawns do not invent
  teeth.
- An expression row with clearly different emotions (happy, worried,
  surprised, sleepy), at least one with the mouth open.
- A size-reference frame when two creatures differ a lot in size (a mouse
  and a bear): both side by side on the same ground line. If the scale is
  ever uncertain in a shot, the smaller one is drawn smaller, never larger.
- Fix a flawed sheet with an edit of that sheet, never by rebuilding it from
  scratch: a rebuilt sheet is a new character.

On Lavendly the sheet comes from the user (Asset Library, then
`import_asset`), from the app, or from an approved scene 1 opening image. The
head close-up is reference 1 in every clip that has slots; the full body is
reference 2 on 4-reference models.

## 2. The creature hero block

Add to the usual hero block: the leg count, how it stands, the tail, the
ears and the eyes.

```text
Pip: a small round red fox cub standing on four legs, oversized pointed ears, cream chest and muzzle, black socks on all four paws, one bushy tail with a white tip, large amber eyes with one white catchlight each, a blue knitted scarf, a white tip on the left ear only.
```

## 3. How animals stand and walk

| Foot type | Who | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| Digitigrade (walks on its toes) | cats, dogs, foxes, wolves, most birds | "walks on its toes, heels high, the hind leg bends forward at the knee and back at the hock" |
| Plantigrade (whole foot down) | bears, raccoons, hedgehogs, mice, rabbits at rest | "places the whole flat foot down with each step" |
| Unguligrade (on hoof tips) | horses, deer, goats, cows | "steps on hard hooves, thin lower legs, the hock bends backward" |

The joint that looks like a backward knee on a hind leg is the ankle (the
hock). Fore legs bend at the elbow, which points backward.

### Gaits

| Gait | Footfall | Feel | Prompt words |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walk | four beats: left hind, left fore, right hind, right fore; two or three feet always down | calm, curious | "walks slowly on all four paws, one paw at a time, two or three paws on the ground at every moment" |
| Trot | two beats: diagonal pairs move together | purposeful | "trots on all four paws, diagonal legs moving together, back level" |
| Canter | three beats, one lead leg | playful, quick | "canters on all four legs with a rocking motion" |
| Gallop | four beats with a moment of all feet off the ground | flight, chase | "gallops on all four legs, body stretching and folding, a moment with all paws off the ground" |
| Bound | hind feet land together ahead of the fore feet | rabbits, squirrels, weasels | "bounds, the hind feet landing together ahead of the front paws" |
| Hop | both hind feet push off together | frogs, kangaroos, small birds on the ground | "hops with both feet together" |
| Pounce | crouch, wiggle, spring, front paws land first | foxes, cats | "crouches low, rear end wiggles once, springs forward and lands front paws first" |

Rules for every four-legged hero:

- "On all four paws, never upright on two legs", unless the brief makes the
  creature walk upright (then say "walks upright on two legs like a person"
  in every shot, for the whole film).
- One gait per clip, named, with its speed ("a slow walk", "a trot").
- Fast gaits warp legs: keep a gallop under 4 s, framed medium wide, side
  view, a locked-off or panning camera.
- Turning in place is the most likely moment for a fifth leg: prefer a turn
  that is cut, or a slow turn with "four legs visible throughout, never more
  than four".
- Feet meet the ground: "each paw presses into the moss and leaves it".

### Other bodies

| Creature | Count and motion to write |
|---|---|
| Bird | two legs, two wings; small birds hop, large birds walk; "two wings folded against the body" when at rest |
| Fish | "swims with side-to-side tail strokes, fins rippling"; no legs |
| Insect | "six legs, three on each side"; wings named when flying |
| Spider | "eight legs"; avoid in kids' films unless the brief wants one, and keep it round and soft |
| Snake | "no legs, moves in S-shaped curves along the ground" |
| Dragon or winged beast | "four legs and two wings, six limbs in all", or "two legs and two wings" (wyvern): pick one and write it every time |
| Robot or object character | name the limbs: "two stubby arms, no legs, rolls on one wheel" |

## 4. Tails, ears, whiskers: emotion without a face

| Emotion | Tail | Ears | Body |
|---|---|---|---|
| Happy, curious | up, gentle swish | forward | light steps |
| Afraid | tucked low | flat back | crouched |
| Alert | still, raised | upright, turning | frozen |
| Sad, tired | hanging low | drooping sideways | slow, head low |
| Playful | high, wagging | forward | front low, rear up |

Animated creatures act with these more than with the face; use them for
one emotion per beat.

## 5. Mouths

- Silent beats keep the mouth closed: "mouth closed, a small calm smile".
- Speech inside a generated clip is the riskiest shot for a creature: teeth
  appear, the jaw stretches, the muzzle melts. Prefer narration with the
  mouth closed. When the creature must speak on screen, one short line of
  up to about 8 words, medium close-up, one speaking face, locked camera,
  and the mouth described: "the muzzle opens and closes softly in time with
  the words, small rounded teeth, the tongue pink".
- Beaks open and close; they never show teeth: "the beak opens and closes,
  no teeth".
- For children, never an open-mouth close-up with many teeth; a yawn is
  "a wide pink yawn with a small rounded tongue".

Mouth shapes animators use, as words for a short line:

| Sound | Shape |
|---|---|
| A, I | jaw open, mouth wide |
| E | mouth wide, teeth close together |
| O | lips round, jaw half open |
| U, W | lips pushed forward, small round opening |
| M, B, P | lips closed, pressed |
| F, V | upper teeth on the lower lip |
| L | tongue tip up behind the teeth |
| Rest | mouth closed, relaxed |

Generated clips do not follow a mouth chart frame by frame; the table helps
pick a line whose key word has a clear, open shape and to judge the result.

## 6. Eyes

- Both eyes look the same way: "both eyes look up at the star together".
- Name the pupil: round (dogs, foxes in stylised films, most heroes),
  vertical slit (cats, a sly fox), horizontal bar (goats, sheep). Keep it
  the same in every prompt.
- One catchlight per eye, on the same side as the key light, in every shot.
- Blinks: "blinks once, slowly" at most once per beat; constant blinking
  reads as a glitch.
- Eye size is a proportion rule in the bible; large eyes with visible
  white around the iris read as friendly in a stylised film, small hard
  pupils with no catchlight read as dead.
- Eyes lead the turn: the eyes move first, the head follows, the body last.

## 7. Failure table

| Failure | Fix in the prompt or the plan |
|---|---|
| Animal stands up and runs on two legs | "on all four paws, a fox's trot, never upright" in every motion prompt with movement |
| Five legs, or legs merging in a turn | slower gait, a cut instead of a turn, "four legs visible throughout, never more than four" |
| Feet sliding on the ground | "each paw lands and stays planted until it lifts", a locked-off camera |
| Two of the hero | "exactly one Pip in frame, nobody else, no copy of him"; no mirrors or water reflections unless bounded |
| Extra tail or ears changing shape | the tail and ear words of the hero block in the still; the head close-up as reference 1 |
| Teeth or a scary open mouth | mouth closed by default; beaks "no teeth"; dialogue moved to narration |
| Eyes looking two ways, uneven size | "both eyes the same size, both looking at the lantern" |
| Fur crawling, texture boiling | lower motion amount, shorter clip, "fur stays still except where the wind moves it" |
| Creature grows larger than a bigger character | the size-reference frame; "the mouse stands no taller than the bear's paw" |
| A new animal appears in a point of view shot | "a point of view through the grass, no animal or person in frame" |
