# Visual realism, physical logic and artefacts

The goal is not photorealism in every film. The goal is a chosen style that
stays coherent and intentional. Stylisation is fine when it is deliberate and
constant.

## 1. Physical continuity, per shot

- Identity: face, age, hair, clothing, accessories; the hero's shape,
  material, colour, size.
- Objects: size, orientation, position, state (on or off, open or closed,
  full or empty).
- Hands: fingers, grip, tool orientation, the contact point.
- Weight, balance, friction, collision, plausible motion.
- Water, smoke, steam, fabric, hair and particles responding to motion and
  wind consistently.
- Mechanisms moving along plausible paths.
- Shadows and reflections agreeing with object positions and the light.
- Background geometry, architecture and props stable unless changed on
  purpose.

## 2. Motion

One primary action per clip. Say:

- the starting state (matching the opening image);
- the action, with timing;
- direction and pace ("at walking pace", "slowly", "in one motion");
- the contact point or interaction;
- the end state;
- what stays still;
- the one camera move, if any.

Motion has acceleration, deceleration and consequence: a hand that touches
a surface meets it, an object does not drift, feet do not slide. Several
transformations, several camera moves and several subject actions in one
short clip produce mush; split them into shots and cut.

## 3. Light, reflection, material

- One source direction and one shadow logic per scene.
- Important reflections on glass, metal, glossy packaging, water and
  polished stone described: what they reflect.
- Matte stays matte, glossy stays glossy.
- Condensation, wetness, steam, dust, smoke only with a cause.
- Texture kept in highlights and shadows where it matters.
- Translucent materials show their edges through reflection, refraction or
  background contrast.

## 4. Depth and focus

- The subject sharp enough for its purpose.
- Shallow focus selectively; context the viewer needs stays visible.
- No focus hunting unless the style asks for it.
- A rack focus has a clear start and destination.
- Background blur consistent with the perspective and focus distance.

## 5. Object permanence

- The subject stays inside the frame during movement.
- No accidental cropping of hands, tools, edges or text that must be read.
- Spatial relationships hold between shots.
- An object that moves has a plausible path.
- Nothing appears, disappears or changes shape without a cut or a reason.

## 6. Text, logos and UI

Generated text is unreliable. When exact words, numbers, logos or interface
labels matter: keep the frame simple, leave the label blank, leave room for
a title, and let the user add the words in the app. Never claim a generated
label is correct without opening the frame. Never invent product UI or brand
details.

## 7. Artefacts to look for in every frame

- Morphing faces, a changing identity between shots.
- Extra or fused fingers, unstable hands.
- Warped labels, logos, text or controls.
- Flickering textures, light or background objects.
- Rubber motion, sliding feet, floating objects.
- Reflections and shadows that disagree.
- Unmotivated camera shake or a drifting perspective.
- Unwanted slow motion, particles, flare, fog.
- Subject scale changing between frames.
- Object state different in adjacent shots.
- Phone UI, battery icons, timestamps, recorder dots, watermarks.

## 8. Repair, in order

1. Name the most damaging visible failure.
2. Simplify the action or the camera move.
3. Reduce the number of interacting objects.
4. Strengthen the anchors: the hero block verbatim, the reference wired, the
   opening image showing the start state, the end state explicit.
5. Split a complex action into another shot.
6. Use the cut (a trim, a dip to black) for what the model cannot do.
7. Regenerate only when the expected improvement is worth the credits.

Never fix a flaw with a longer negative list. Write a clearer positive
instruction.

## 9. Style consistency

Keep the chosen level of realism, colour, contrast, texture, grain and
motion language constant across the film. Film grain, bloom, anamorphic
flare and heavy grading only when the brief asks, and then in every shot.
