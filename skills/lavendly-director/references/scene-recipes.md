# Scene recipes by situation

Starting points, not templates. Each gives the goal, a coverage pattern that
fits 3 to 5 scenes of 4 to 8 s, the light, the sound and what to watch. Adapt
to the hero, the place and the brief. Scene 1 is always the hook.

## 1. Product reveal (for example a thermos, a bottle, a watch)

Goal: considered, desirable, tactile; the same object in every frame.

- Scene 1, hook: a detail or a controlled glimpse (for example the dent
  on a cap, a highlight on a shoulder, the grain of brushed steel). Macro or close, locked-off or a slow push-in.
- Scene 2, context: the product in its world (a nook, a bathroom shelf, a
  wrist), medium or wide, one practical light, the object doing its one thing.
- Scene 3, hero: three-quarter angle on a clean background, the full form,
  space for a title. Locked-off or a quarter orbit.
- Light: broad controlled gradients on gloss, narrow edges on glass and
  metal; the product still, the camera or the light moving.
- Sound: one physical sound (a click, a cap, cloth on wood), room tone,
  score low under narration.
- Watch: the hero block verbatim, labels left blank, reflections named,
  no second copy of the product.

## 2. Food and cooking

Goal: actions understandable, food tactile.

- Establish the workspace and ingredients; overhead for arrangement, three
  quarter or side for hands and depth.
- Inserts at meaningful points: knife contact, ingredient falling, sizzle,
  pour, steam, garnish. One per clip.
- Build preparation, cooking, plating, reveal. Never disconnected food shots.
- Light: side or back-side for steam and texture; believable colour.
- Sound: real Foley in sync with the contact: chop, sizzle, pour, plate.
- Watch: ingredient amounts, utensil positions and hand placement match
  across cuts.

## 3. Car and mobility

Goal: design, motion, performance or lifestyle.

- Wide environmental shot first for place and scale.
- Low tracking near the wheels only where plausible; close-ups on lights,
  bodywork, interior controls; one static hero shot so the sequence breathes.
- Light: reflections travel consistently across the body as the camera or
  the car moves.
- Sound: road, engine or motor as the vehicle really sounds.
- Watch: wheel rotation, road motion, vehicle direction, shadows; the same
  car in every shot.

## 4. Interior design and renovation

Goal: space, progress and transformation legible.

- The original room from a repeatable angle; the problem details; inserts of
  materials, tools, joins, hands doing one step; the finished space by a
  slow reveal or a lateral move; the same wide angle again for the after.
- Light: window direction and exterior exposure constant across scenes.
- Watch: room geometry, windows, doors, stairs and furniture stay put.

## 5. Explainer and "did you know"

Goal: a fact understood and remembered.

- Hook: a surprising visual, a direct question, a clear contrast.
- Evidence or a concrete example, not decorative footage under narration;
  close-ups for detail, wide for context.
- Narration carries the facts; captions on; one style plate keeps the look
  (flat illustration, a lab, a kitchen).
- Sound: restrained accents on the key reveal; narration never masked.
- End on the takeaway, not a montage.

## 6. Emotional dialogue

Goal: a relationship, a tension, a change of feeling.

- Two-shot or medium wide for geography; eye-level medium close-ups for the
  lines; reactions and listening, not only the speaker.
- Screen direction and the conversational axis kept.
- Small framing or focus changes on emotional turns; no push on every line.
- Light motivated by windows or practicals, constant across coverage.
- Sound: the lines in the clip's own audio, one per clip, with the delivery
  written; room tone; pauses carry meaning; score very low or absent.

## 7. Suspense

Goal: control what the viewer knows and when.

- A readable location first, then information restricted: negative space,
  foreground obstruction, off-screen sound.
- Slow motivated reveal or stillness over handheld motion.
- Light from plausible sources, enough to read the key action.
- Sound: room tone, distant movement, mechanical sounds, selective silence.
- The payoff visually clear; concealment builds anticipation, not confusion.

## 8. Travel and destination

Goal: place, atmosphere, a reason to care.

- A strong establishing view, then human-scale details: street life, craft,
  food, architecture, one small observed moment.
- Natural light and local ambience; scale cues (people, doors, trees).
- One time of day and one weather per continuous sequence.
- A sound bridge between locations when it helps.
- End on a memorable view or a human moment, not a random montage.

## 9. Fashion and portrait

Goal: personality, silhouette, texture, styling.

- Framing that serves the garment or the expression; full body for
  silhouette, inserts for seams, jewellery, material.
- Controlled movement: a turn, a walk, fabric moving, a camera slide.
- Light that reveals fabric and face without flattening texture.
- Watch: fit, pattern, accessories, hair and makeup identical across shots;
  wind direction consistent.

## 10. Documentary and nature

Goal: observe rather than stage.

- Habitat first, then behaviour, then detail. Patient locked-off or restrained
  tracking; telephoto for distance, wide for environment.
- Natural movement, scale, weather and behaviour respected.
- Sound: authentic ambience, minimal score, narration spare.
- Watch: never imply the footage documents a real event.

## 11. Craft and satisfying process

Goal: process and material feedback satisfying to watch.

- The object and the tool clearly before the action; the point of contact
  framed; macro inserts for texture.
- Raw material through visible stages to a finished result; each clip
  continues the previous state (glass still glowing, clay still wet).
- Light: the material's own glow or one hard side light for relief.
- Sound: tactile Foley in sync with contact; no score, or almost none.
- Watch: tool orientation, hand position and material state match between
  shots.

## 12. Software and app demonstration

Goal: value clear, interface readable.

- The user's problem first; the real interface, never an invented one.
- One idea per card or callout; cursor and transitions legible.
- Generated UI is unreliable: use real screen captures imported as assets
  and keep generated shots for the human context around them.

## 13. Music performance and artist visual

Goal: movement, mood and phrasing connected.

- Wide, medium and close coverage that fits the performance; cuts and light
  changes tied to the musical structure, not every beat.
- Silhouettes and backlight when the mood calls for it.
- Watch: instrument handling, body movement, light direction and stage
  geography.

## 14. Before and after

Goal: the change measurable and satisfying.

- The before from a repeatable angle; what will change and what must stay
  fixed named; meaningful intermediate steps if the process has value.
- Matched framing for the comparison; a hard cut or a dip to black.
- No camera or light change that exaggerates the transformation.

## 15. Any new situation

1. The viewer's question or need.
2. The clearest establishing view.
3. The key action or information.
4. One or two details that add understanding or sensory impact.
5. Light that fits setting and subject.
6. Movement only where it adds information or feeling.
7. Sound and the transition out.
8. Continuity anchors and the likely failures.
9. Remove every redundant shot.
