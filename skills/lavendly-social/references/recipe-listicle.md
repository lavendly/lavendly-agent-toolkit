# Recipe: listicles and top-N

"Top 5", "3 things I", "ranked", "worst to best": a numbered list where each
item is one beat with the same treatment. Worked example: five kitchen
tools a professional cook replaced first (the user's list).

## 1. Purpose, audience, ending

- Purpose: useful or entertaining items, each worth the wait, with the best
  one last.
- Audience: savers and sharers; lists are saved for later and sent to
  friends.
- The ending is number one, held longest, then one line (a question or a
  loop). The promise of the hook ("number one surprised me") must be kept.

## 2. Format rules

- Order: countdown (five to one) keeps viewers to the end; count-up (one to
  five) suits steps. The strongest item is last in a countdown, first in a
  count-up only when the hook shows it.
- Set parity: every item gets the same treatment (shot size, light, move
  direction pattern, duration, spoken number first). Different treatment
  makes some items look like afterthoughts.
- Camera rhythm for lists: the same move for each item with the direction
  flipped each time (push-in, push-in from the other side, or truck left
  then truck right), and one different move for number one.
- The number is spoken first: "Number three." The user adds on-screen
  numbers in the app if wanted.
- Honest criteria: say what the ranking is based on ("ranked by how often I
  use them"). Product lists with affiliate links need the platform's
  disclosure; no fake ratings or reviews.

## 3. Structures (9:16, Veo 3.1 Fast)

### 30 s (top 5)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### 45 to 60 s (top 7 or 10)

Each item 5 s, a hook of 3 s, a closing 3 to 5 s; ten items fit in 60 s
only at 5 s each with one line apiece (about 10 to 12 words). Offer top 5
in 30 s first; long lists lose viewers in the middle unless every third
item is a surprise.

## 4. Hook and payoff

- Hook: the promise and the count ("Five tools I replaced first. Number one
  is cheap."), over a frame of number one in action or of the old thing
  being thrown out.
- Mid re-hook at item three: "This next one changed how I cook."
- Payoff: number one, the longest and the only static or different move,
  then a question: "What would be your number one?"

## 5. Camera, light, colour

- Items: "top-down, camera pointing straight down over a worn butcher
  block", the same height and light for every item; "close-up from a low
  angle" for number one only.
- Light: "soft daylight from a window on the left, a warm under-cabinet
  light"; identical light line in every item's still prompt.
- Tools are plain and unbranded: "a plain steel bench scraper with a
  wooden handle".

## 6. Sound

- Voice: punchy, 2.5 words per second at most; brian, jessica or chris.
  Each item: number, name, one reason (10 to 11 words).
- Native: the tool's sound (scrape, grate, sizzle) `mix` 0.4 under the
  voice; this is the satisfying layer.
- Bed: "Upbeat funk bass and light hand percussion at about 105 BPM, a
  short drum fill into each new number, drops out for number one, back for
  the last line." Volume 0.3, ducking on.
- Captions: `tiktok`.

## 8. Prompt pairs (Veo 3.1 Fast)

**Pair 1, an item (number four, the microplane).**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Clean studio tabletop film, soft even light. Two hands hold a plain steel microplane grater with a black handle angled over a worn butcher block, a wedge of parmesan in the right hand touching the blade. Top-down, camera pointing straight down, 35mm look, deep focus. Soft daylight from a window on the left, a warm under-cabinet light. Worn wood, pale cheese, steel. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Clean studio tabletop film, soft even light, one continuous take. Top-down. 0 to 4 s: the cheese slides down the grater in three firm strokes, already in motion from the first frame, and a fine white snow of parmesan falls into a small mound on the block. Camera: slow truck right, stopping with the mound centred. End state: a neat mound of grated cheese, the grater lifted. The hands, the block and the light stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly two hands. SFX: three crisp grating strokes. No music. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Pair 2, number one.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Clean studio tabletop film, soft even light. A plain steel bench scraper with a wooden handle rests flat on a butcher block beside a messy pile of chopped green herbs and onion. Close-up from a low angle at board height, 50mm look, shallow depth of field. Soft daylight from the left, a warm under-cabinet light. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Clean studio tabletop film, soft even light, one continuous take. Close-up, low angle. 0 to 2 s: a hand grips the scraper and sweeps the whole pile across the board in one stroke. 2 to 5 s: the scraper lifts the pile and tips it off the edge of the board out of frame; the board is clean. 5 to 8 s: the scraper rests flat again; nothing moves. Camera: slow push-in, then holds still from 5 s. End state: a clean board, the scraper flat. The board and the light stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one hand. SFX: one long scrape, a soft tap. No music. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Second family, Seedance 2.0 Mini.** "STARTING STATE / TIMELINE / CAMERA /
AUDIO / ENDING STATE" with the grating strokes "already in motion from the
first frame"; 5 s per item.

## 9. Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Items look different in light and angle | One light line and one camera height pasted in every item |
| A brand name or logo on a tool | "plain", "unbranded", "with a black handle" |
| The tool morphs while used | One stroke pattern, the contact point named, a slow action |
| Number one is no better than the rest | Reorder; give number one the different move and the longest hold |
| The list drags in the middle | Cut to five items; 5 s each; a re-hook at item three |
| A rating or score drawn in frame | Spoken only |

## 10. Policy guards

- Rankings say their basis; no invented ratings, test results or reviews.
- Affiliate or sponsored items: the user turns on the platform's
  disclosure; the voice can say "I get a commission if you buy".
- No real brands' logos generated; real products the user names are shown
  as plain generic objects unless the user supplies real photos.
