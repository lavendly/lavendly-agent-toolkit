# Recipe: storytime

A first-person story told by a voice, with pictures that reconstruct it.
The voice is the show; the pictures keep the eyes. Worked example: "The
night I got locked in a museum" (the user's own story, or a clearly
fictional one).

## 1. Purpose, audience, ending

- Purpose: a story with a hook, rising trouble, a turn and a button line,
  told in under a minute.
- Audience: viewers who stay for a voice and a twist; comments are where
  the story lives on.
- The ending is the button: one short line after the resolution that lands
  the joke or the feeling ("The guard still sends me a card every
  Christmas."). Then cut.

## 2. Format rules

- The voice leads at about 2.5 words per second (story pace), one voice,
  conversational, contractions, short sentences. ElevenLabs delivery tags
  carry performance sparingly: `[whispers]` on the scary line, `[laughs]`
  once at most.
- Pictures reconstruct, they do not illustrate every word: one picture per
  beat, mostly places, objects, hands and the narrator-character from
  behind or at distance. The narrator's face, if shown, is a fictional
  character consistent across shots.
- Phone-footage realism or a "night reconstruction" look; one imperfection
  per shot.
- Truth: the user's own story told as theirs is fine. A made-up story is
  labelled as fiction. Never a fabricated "true story" about real,
  identifiable people or places, never a story that accuses a real person.

## 3. Story shape (beat menu)

| Beat | Share of runtime | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0 to 3 s | The most extreme moment or line, out of order ("I spent a night locked in a museum.") |
| Setup | 10 to 15 percent | Who, where, why it seemed normal |
| Trouble 1 | 15 percent | The first thing that goes wrong |
| Trouble 2 | 15 percent | Worse |
| Turn | 15 to 20 percent | The surprise or the decision |
| Resolution | 15 percent | How it ended |
| Button | last 2 to 3 s | One line that reframes or lands it |

### 60 s

The 30 s shape with each trouble beat doubled (two shots), a longer turn
(the misunderstanding with the guard), and one held moment at the centre
(8 s, the dark gallery with only breathing and footsteps). About 18 clips,
about 140 words.

### 90 s

A two-part story: part one ends on the turn ("And then the torch found
me."); part two starts with a one-line recap. Two films of 45 s beat one of
90 s.

## 5. Hook and payoff

- Hook: the story's most extreme line first, out of order, spoken from
  0.0 s over the most legible frame of that moment.
- Payoff: the resolution shown, then the button line over a quiet frame.
  Let the last word land with half a second of silence.

## 6. Camera, light, colour

- Reconstruction look: "Phone video, handheld on a phone rear camera, night,
  low light, soft noise in the shadows" or "Night reconstruction, moonlight
  through high windows, one torch beam".
- The narrator-character: seen from behind, hands, silhouettes; when the
  face is shown, a fixed description and a reference.
- Light motivated by the story: moonlight, exit sign glow (sign blank),
  torch beam, dawn.
- Colour: "cold blue moonlight, warm torch, sodium orange at dawn".

## 7. Sound

- Voice: the whole script on clip 1; jessica, liam or charlie for a young
  storyteller, bill or lily for a warmer older register; tags sparingly.
- Native: footsteps, door clicks, the echo of a big room; `mix` 0.4 under
  the voice; one `native` moment where the voice pauses.
- Bed: "Sparse plucked strings and a soft low pulse at about 80 BPM,
  tension rising under the troubles, dropping to silence for the turn,
  resolving warm under the ending." Volume 0.25, ducking on.
- Captions: `tiktok`; the story is often watched with sound, but captions
  hold muted viewers.

## 9. Prompt pairs (Veo 3.1 Fast)

**Pair 1, the hook.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Phone video still, handheld on a phone rear camera, night. A tall dark wooden gallery door with brass handles stands slightly open onto a dim museum corridor, moonlight from high windows on a stone floor. Close-up on the gap between the door leaves, phone wide lens. Cold blue moonlight, deep shadows, soft noise in the dark areas. No signs, no lettering. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Phone video, one continuous take, unstabilised handheld phone camera, night. Close-up. 0 to 2 s: the door leaves swing shut by themselves, already in motion from the first frame. 2 to 3 s: a heavy lock clicks; the frame jolts slightly. 3 to 4 s: stillness, only moonlight. Camera: handheld sway, a small jolt at the click. End state: the doors closed, a thin line of moonlight under them. The doors, the floor and the light stay as in the opening frame. No person in frame. SFX: a heavy wooden thud, a metal lock click, echo. No music. Clean frame: no text, no recording dot, no timestamp, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Pair 2, the turn.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Phone video still, handheld, night. A long dark museum gallery with empty stone plinths in moonlight, a corner at the far end lit faintly from behind by a torch beam. First-person view at head height, phone wide lens. Cold blue moonlight, one warm torch glow at the corner. No signs, no lettering. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Phone video, one continuous take, first-person view at head height, unstabilised. 0 to 2 s: a torch beam sweeps around the far corner across the plinths. 2 to 4 s: the beam swings toward the camera and fills the frame with warm glare; the exposure drops, then recovers. Camera: a small step back, then still. End state: the beam pointed at the lens, the gallery dark around it. The gallery and the moonlight stay as in the opening frame. No face visible, only the beam. SFX: approaching footsteps on stone, a keyring jingle. No music. Clean frame: no text, no recording dot, no timestamp, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Second family, Seedance 2.0 Mini.** "STARTING STATE / TIMELINE / CAMERA /
AUDIO / ENDING STATE", "already in motion from the first frame"; low light
words: "night, cold moonlight, soft noise, phone camera".

## 10. Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Pictures illustrate every word literally | One picture per beat; the voice carries detail |
| The night is too dark to read | Name the moonlight and one practical; "deep shadows with the floor readable" |
| Readable signs (exit, gallery names) | "No signs, no lettering"; signs blank |
| The narrator-character's face changes | Show from behind, hands, silhouette; or a reference with its role sentence |
| The voice runs past the last picture | Cut words; the button line under 2 s |
| Art on the walls copies real paintings | "plain dark canvases in heavy frames, seen from the side" |

## 11. Policy guards

- True stories are the user's own; fiction is labelled fiction.
- No real person accused, named or shown; no real institution shown as a
  place where something bad happened.
- Generated people are characters; never presented as the real people of a
  real event.
- No copyrighted artworks reproduced in the set.
