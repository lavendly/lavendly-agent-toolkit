# Recipe: UGC-style ads

Ads that look filmed on a phone by a person, not by a studio: selfie
demos, hands-only unboxings, quick reviews, ASMR handling, small skits. The
look earns trust only if it stays honest: an AI person in a UGC-style ad
is an actor demonstrating a product, never a customer reporting results.

## 1. The rule and the disclosure line

- No AI person is ever presented as a real customer. They show the
  product, say what it is and how it is used, and react to the moment
  (the texture, the click). They never claim personal results, time of use,
  or that they bought it.
- A real customer's words may be performed by an AI person only with the
  customer's permission, verbatim, and labelled as a dramatisation.
- Disclosure line, every UGC-style ad with an AI person: "Dramatisation.
  AI-generated person, not a real customer." It goes in the post caption
  (lavendly-publish) and as an on-screen label the user adds in the app.
  Tell the user both at delivery; turn on the platform's AI-content label
  where the placement offers one.
- Hands-only and product-only formats need no person at all: prefer them.

| Line type | Allowed for an AI person | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | yes | "It's a gel, not a cream." |
| How to use it | yes | "Two pumps. Pat, don't rub." |
| Sensory reaction in the moment | yes | "Oh, that's cold." |
| Facts the user supplied | yes, verbatim | "Fragrance free, thirty millilitres." |
| Personal results | no | "My skin cleared in two weeks." |
| Time of use, purchase, loyalty | no | "I've used it for a month." |
| Social proof | no | "Everyone keeps asking me about it." |

## 2. Purpose and ending

- Audience: cold audiences on TikTok and Reels who scroll past polish.
- The viewer should feel they watched someone show them a thing in their
  own bathroom or kitchen.
- The ending: the product in hand or on a real shelf, the call to action
  spoken in the presenter's voice or by narration ("Link's below."), held 2
  s, the disclosure label added by the user.

## 3. The five formats

| Format | Picture | People | Best length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product review (demo) | selfie talking head plus hand inserts | one AI presenter, demonstrative lines only | 15 to 30 s |
| Unboxing | top-down hands open a box, lift the product, turn it to the lens | hands only | 15 s |
| ASMR | close, quiet handling: cap, pump, pour, texture | hands only | 15 to 30 s |
| Entertainment skit | a small staged moment with the product (a knock-over saved, a race to grab it) | AI actors, obviously staged | 15 to 30 s |
| Street interview | a person stopped on a street | only as a labelled skit; prefer another format | avoid |

## 4. Phone-footage realism

Decide first, and write it into the still and the motion prompt: who
filmed it (the presenter, a friend, her own hand), on what (a phone's front
or back camera), when (morning bathroom, kitchen after work). Then give
every shot exactly one deliberate imperfection, never the same twice:

| Imperfection | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Late framing | "the camera catches up a beat late as she lifts the tube" |
| Focus hunt | "focus hunts for half a second, then locks on the tube" |
| Exposure shift | "exposure brightens as she turns toward the window" |
| Hand sway | "handheld with a faint natural sway" |
| Off-centre | "her face slightly left of centre, the top of her head cropped" |
| Background life | "a kettle clicks off in the background" |
| Glance away | "she glances off camera once, then back" |
| Small bump | "the tube taps the lens once and the frame jolts slightly" |

The look words: "vertical phone video look, front camera, slight
wide-angle distortion, natural uneven window light, auto exposure". Always
the clean-frame line: phone looks pull in battery icons, record dots and
timestamps unless the prompt rules them out.

## 5. Structure by length

Gen is the length to generate on Veo (4 s; spoken clips 4 to 8 s). On
Seedance generate 5. "Photo" means the scene opens on the user's photo of
the product on a real shelf, counter or bag.

**6 s**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 to 3 | medium close-up selfie, front camera | handheld sway | hook: she holds the tube to the lens: "Okay, look at this." | 3 | 4 |
| 2 | 3 to 6 | close-up of the shelf, back camera | handheld | the product on the shelf; narration "Link's below." | 3 | 4, photo |

**15 s** (product review, demonstrative)

| # | Time | Size, angle, lens feel | Move | Action | Cut | Gen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**60 s**: two or three demos (texture, use, carry), one unboxing, the facts
the user supplied, the call to action. Keep each spoken clip 4 to 8 s with
one line, cut with hand inserts between; 12 to 15 shots, 16 to 20 spoken
words per 15 s of talking.

## 6. Hook and payoff

- Hook: a face already mid-sentence holding the product toward the lens,
  the product bumping the lens, a box being slit open, or a sound (the cap
  pop). A direct callout of the audience works ("Desk workers, look.").
- Payoff: the product working in her hands (the texture holding, the
  click, the fit in the pocket), then the shelf shot and the call to action.

## 7. Camera, light, colour

- Camera: front camera for selfies at arm's length (medium close-up, face
  in the upper-middle band), back camera for hands and the shelf; handheld
  with a faint sway, never a gimbal glide; jump cuts between clips.
- Light: what a bathroom or kitchen has: "soft window light from the left,
  a warm ceiling light behind", "the ring of a small vanity light reflected
  in her eyes". No studio key.
- Colour: phone-native, slightly warm, no grade; real rooms with a little
  clutter named ("a toothbrush cup, a folded towel").

## 8. Sound

- Spoken lines are native dialogue in the clip: `native` 1, no narration
  over them. Two words per second of clip at most (a 4 s clip holds 8
  words), one speaker per clip, her face in frame, "(no subtitles)" after
  the line on Veo.
- Narration only for the call to action over the shelf shot, in a voice
  close to hers, or none.
- Score: "Light lo-fi bed around 90 BPM, soft keys and a muted kick, low
  under the voice, a small rise on the call to action." Volume 0.15 to 0.2
  with ducking, or none: UGC often runs on room sound alone.
- Mix: spoken clips `native` 1; hand inserts `native` 0.8; the shelf shot
  `mix` 0.4 under the narration line.

## 10. Prompt pairs (Veo)

**Pair 1: the selfie hook**

Still:

```text
Vertical phone photo, front camera, slight wide-angle distortion. A woman in her late twenties with a messy bun, an oversized grey hoodie and no makeup, in a small bright bathroom, holding a plain white squeeze tube close to the lens beside her cheek. Soft window light from the left, a warm ceiling light behind. Natural uneven skin texture. A toothbrush cup and a folded towel behind her. No text, no logo, no on-screen graphics.
```

Motion:

```text
Handheld phone camera with a faint natural sway; the tube taps the lens once at the start and the frame jolts slightly. She pulls the tube back to show it and says, casually: Okay, look at this texture. (no subtitles) She glances off camera once, then back at the lens. Her face, hoodie and the bathroom stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one woman in frame. Ambient noise: bathroom echo, a fan humming. No music. Clean frame with no on-screen graphics, no battery icon, no timestamp, no record dot.
```

**Pair 2: hands-only unboxing**

Still:

```text
Vertical phone photo from above, back camera. Two hands rest on a small kraft cardboard box with a strip of brown tape across the lid, on a pale kitchen counter with a fruit bowl at the edge. Morning window light from the top of frame, soft shadows. Natural, slightly cluttered. No text, no logo, no printing on the box.
```

Motion:

```text
Handheld top-down phone camera, a faint sway. 0 to 2 s: a thumbnail slits the tape along the lid. 2 to 4 s: the hands lift the lid and fold back white tissue paper; the camera catches up a beat late. The box, the counter and the light stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly two hands, one box. SFX: tape tearing, cardboard rustle, tissue paper. No music. Clean frame with no on-screen graphics, no timestamps.
```

**Pair 3: ASMR cap and pump**

Still:

```text
Vertical phone photo, back camera, close-up. A plain white pump bottle held in one hand above a white bathroom sink, the cap already off in the other hand. Soft window light from the left, a few water drops on the basin. Natural light, no styling. No text, no logo, no label lettering.
```

Motion:

```text
Handheld phone camera, very close, a faint sway; focus hunts for half a second, then locks on the nozzle. 0 to 2 s: one press of the pump; a small bead of clear gel lands on two fingertips. 2 to 4 s: the fingertips rub together slowly. The bottle and the hands stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one bottle, two hands. SFX: the pump click, a soft wet sound. Ambient noise: quiet bathroom. No music. Clean frame, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Second families.** Seedance 2.0 Fast for hands-only: a production note,
"already in motion from the first frame", reference 1 as the product's
shape and colours only. Kling v3 Standard for non-English lines: the
labelled form `[Woman, warm casual voice]: "line"` with the action first
and no pronouns.

## 11. Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Over-polished, studio look | "phone front camera, natural uneven light, no styling"; one imperfection per shot |
| Phone UI, battery, record dot, timestamp | the full clean-frame line every time |
| Lip sync drifts | one line of 8 words or fewer per 4 s clip, one speaking face, medium close-up, a faint sway only |
| Garbled subtitles in a Veo clip | "(no subtitles)" after the line; captions from narration only |
| The product changes in her hand | the shelf shot opens on the real photo; hand shots keep it partly turned; the reference wired |
| A second person appears in a mirror | keep mirrors out of frame |
| The same face, different voice per clip | one delivery label in every spoken clip ("casually, warm, mid-pitch") |

## 12. Policy guards

- The rule and the disclosure line in section 1, every time.
- No fake reviews, star ratings, follower counts, "sold out" claims or
  invented testimonials.
- No minors as presenters; no real creators' likeness or catchphrases.
- Beauty, health and cleaning: no results claimed by anyone in the ad.
