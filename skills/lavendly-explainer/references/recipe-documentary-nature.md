# Recipe: documentary and nature

Observe rather than stage: animals, places, processes and people at work,
with natural sound up front and sparse narration. Worked example: a red
fox hunting mice under snow.

## 1. Purpose, audience, ending

- Purpose: the viewer watches a real pattern of the world unfold and
  understands it, without being told more than the pictures show.
- Audience: nature and documentary viewers on YouTube and TV-like screens
  (16:9), and single-behaviour cut-downs on Shorts.
- The ending returns to the place, quieter: what is known, said plainly,
  over a wide shot held long enough to breathe (4 to 8 s).

## 2. Honesty first (it shapes every choice)

- AI nature footage is a reconstruction of typical behaviour, not a
  recording. The film never says or implies "filmed in", "captured", "rare
  footage". The post caption carries a line such as "AI-generated
  reconstruction of typical red fox behaviour."
- Behaviour shown is documented behaviour of that species in its habitat,
  described precisely (the fox listens, leaps high, dives head first: that
  is how red foxes hunt under snow). Never an invented or impossible act.
- Places are generic habitats ("a birch forest in deep snow"), never a named
  reserve on a named day.
- People in a human-interest documentary are illustrative and anonymous
  (seen at work, from behind, hands, at distance); real interviews and real
  people come only from the user's imported footage, with their consent.

Narration about 110 words at 2.1 per second, with long gaps for natural
sound; no line over shots 4, 6 and 9.

### 90 s

Add a second character and a challenge (a raven landing to watch, two
shots of 4 s), a time-lapse transition (shadows sweeping across the snow,
8 s), and a third attempt in a new place (3 shots). About 160 words; more
silence, not more words.

## 4. Hook and payoff

- Hook: the most striking behaviour or an extreme close-up of a sense at
  work (ears turning, an eye opening), with natural sound at 0.0 s.
  Narration may start after one or two seconds of ambience.
- Payoff: the behaviour succeeds or the cycle completes (failure first,
  then success is the classic shape), then a quiet wide.

## 5. Camera, light, colour

- Observational grammar: "static tripod", "long telephoto lens, background
  compressed and soft", "slow pan following the animal at its pace",
  "camera at the animal's eye height", "high aerial drone shot slowly
  advancing" for the establishing wide, "macro slider move" for details.
  No orbits around animals; no handheld unless it is a human documentary.
- Animals unaware of the camera: "looking off to the left, unaware of the
  camera".
- Light, natural only: "soft overcast winter light, pale blue shadows",
  "low golden sun backlighting the fur", "dappled light through leaves",
  "blue hour after sunset". One light per sequence; a change of light gets
  a time-passage shot.
- Colour: "natural colours, rich earth tones, slightly cool shadows"; no
  stylised grade. Human documentary: "16mm film look, fine grain" only when
  the brief asks for it.
- Pacing: average shot 4 to 8 s; hold behaviour shots; cuts every 5 s or
  slower.

## 6. Sound

- Natural sound is the backbone. Clips without narration stay `native`;
  clips under narration `mix` at 0.4 to 0.5 so wind and snow stay audible.
  Every motion prompt names the ambience ("Ambient noise: light wind in
  bare birches, the soft creak of snow") and ends with "No music."
- Narration: calm, present tense, 2.1 words per second, long gaps; george,
  bill or lily. Per-scene voiceovers do not duck a film-wide score. Either
  make the narration one film-wide track (it ducks the score; place the
  lines with the gaps in the script), or keep per-scene lines and set the
  score low (about 20 to 25 percent) and tell the user why.
- Score: "Sparse and cold: a low cello drone and a few soft piano notes at
  about 60 BPM. Enters after the first behaviour, stays under the wind,
  swells slightly on the successful leap, fades out before the final wide
  so only the wind remains." Volume 0.2, ducking on.
- Captions: `youtube` on 16:9 (six words per chunk, calm); `reels` on
  vertical cut-downs.

## 8. Prompt pairs (Veo 3.1 Fast)

Subject block, verbatim in every still prompt: "a red fox with a thick
orange winter coat, white chest and throat, black legs, a full white-tipped
tail".

**Pair 1, listening.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Nature documentary, observational, natural light. A red fox with a thick orange winter coat, white chest and throat, black legs and a full white-tipped tail stands in deep fresh snow in a birch forest, head tilted, looking down to the left, unaware of the camera. Wide shot from a low angle at the fox's height, long telephoto lens, background compressed and soft. Soft overcast winter light, pale blue shadows. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Nature documentary, observational, natural light, one continuous take. Wide shot, low angle, long telephoto lens. 0 to 3 s: the fox stands still; only its ears turn, first left, then forward. 3 to 6 s: it lowers its head a little further and lifts one front paw, holding it in the air. 6 to 8 s: it holds that pose. Camera: static tripod, no movement. End state: head low, one paw raised, ears forward. The fox, the birches and the light stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one fox, no other animal, no person. Ambient noise: light wind in bare birches, near silence. No music. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Pair 2, the leap.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Nature documentary, observational, natural light. A red fox with a thick orange winter coat, white chest and throat, black legs and a full white-tipped tail crouches in deep fresh snow in an open clearing, body coiled, head low, ears forward. Medium shot from a low angle, side view, long telephoto lens, background of snowy birch trunks compressed and soft. Soft overcast winter light. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Nature documentary, observational, natural light, one continuous take. Medium shot, side view, telephoto. 0 to 1 s: the fox pushes off its back legs. 1 to 2 s: it rises in a high arc, front paws together, nose pointing down. 2 to 3 s: it dives head first into the snow up to its shoulders; snow sprays up and falls. 3 to 4 s: only its back legs and tail show above the snow, still. Camera: static tripod, no movement. End state: the fox's hind half above the snow, the spray settling. The clearing and the light stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one fox, four legs. SFX: a soft thump into snow. Ambient noise: wind. No music. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Second family, Wan 2.7.**

```text
The fox pushes off its back legs, rises in a high arc and dives head first into the snow; snow sprays up and settles. Static shot. Medium shot, side, long-focus lens, overcast soft light, low contrast, cool colour tone. Exactly one fox. Sound: soft thump into snow, wind. No music.
```

For a human documentary (a glassblower, a fisherman at dawn), the same
grammar with "workplace documentary, natural window light", the person
anonymous: "seen from behind and in profile, face turned to the work".

## 9. Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Five legs, two tails, legs melting in a leap | Medium-wide framing, side view, the motion path written step by step, "four legs" |
| The animal looks at the lens and poses | "unaware of the camera, looking off to the left" |
| Telephoto look missing (wide-angle distortion on a wild animal) | "long telephoto lens, background compressed and soft", camera far away |
| Calendar-photo colour, oversaturated | "natural colours, soft contrast, slightly cool shadows" |
| A second fox or a person appears | "Exactly one fox, no other animal, no person" in every motion prompt |
| Invented behaviour | Rewrite to the species' documented behaviour |
| Music bed baked into the clip | "No music."; `mix` or `native` without a music track in the clip |
| Snow physics look like flour or foam | "dry powder snow sprays up and falls back", short action, slow pace |

## 10. Policy guards

- Disclose: the post caption says the footage is an AI-generated
  reconstruction; the narration never claims a recording.
- No AI footage presented as a specific real event (a named flood, a named
  animal rescue, a real person's encounter).
- Endangered species and real conservation claims come from the user's
  sources; no invented population numbers.
- Real interviews and real people only from imported footage with consent.
