# Recipe: corporate training

A procedure, policy or skill taught to staff: onboarding, safety, process,
compliance, soft skills. Learning platforms, intranets, team channels.
Worked example: lifting a box safely (the steps in every example stand in
for the user's own policy).

## 1. Purpose, audience, ending

- Purpose: one learning objective met per film ("After this, you can lift
  a box from the floor without strain."). Several objectives make several
  films.
- Audience: employees who must watch; attention is not guaranteed and
  accessibility is required (captions, plain language, sound-off friendly).
- The ending recaps the steps in order and asks one knowledge-check
  question aloud; the answer is in the film.

## 2. Rules that shape the film

- The content comes from the user's policy, procedure or subject expert.
  The agent never invents a safety step, a legal rule or a threshold;
  missing steps are a question, not a guess.
- One objective, three to six steps, each step one shot with its result
  visible.
- Show the right way; show a wrong way only when the policy names a common
  mistake, briefly, and say it is wrong ("Not like this.").
- People are fictional employees, presented as a dramatisation; never a
  real employee's likeness without their written consent, never a real
  person presented as giving a statement. Keep one locked description per
  character and a reference image for consistency.
- No readable screens, forms, signs or logos from the model. Real software
  steps are screen recordings the user imports (see recipe-how-to.md
  section 9). The company logo is added by the user in the app.
- Plain words, second person, short sentences; define a term once.

in his thirties with short dark hair, a navy work polo, grey work trousers,
black safety boots and an orange high-visibility vest".

### 90 s

Add a scenario chapter (25 s: a heavier box, Tariq decides to use a trolley;
three shots) and a second wrong-way example only if the policy names it.
About 190 words. Longer modules become a series of 60 to 90 s films, one
objective each.

## 4. Hook and payoff

- Hook: the objective in the viewer's words ("In one minute: how to lift
  without hurting your back."), or the cost of getting it wrong, said
  plainly. No scare footage.
- Payoff: the recap in order, then one question ("What moves when you
  turn: your feet or your back?").

## 5. Camera, light, colour

- Style line: "Workplace documentary, natural window light, handheld
  steady" for culture and soft skills; "Clean studio tabletop film, soft
  even light" for procedures at a bench.
- Every step locked-off, side view for posture, front view for feet, an
  insert for the hands; same angle for the same kind of step.
- Light: "high skylights, soft daylight, even across the aisle". No harsh
  fluorescent green.
- Colour: "true colours, the company's accent colour once in the set (a
  crate, a chair)"; safety colours accurate (hi-vis orange, yellow lines).

## 6. Sound

- Narration second person, 2.3 words per second; will, sarah or roger.
- Native `mix` 0.3 (warehouse ambience, a box set down).
- Score: "Light, steady and unobtrusive: soft electric piano and a muted
  beat at about 95 BPM, constant through the steps, a single lift on the
  recap, ending cleanly." Volume 0.2, ducking on. Or no score for strict
  compliance modules.
- Captions: `caption` (boxed, four words per chunk) by default for
  accessibility; `youtube` when the user prefers open captions.

## 8. Prompt pairs (Kling v3 Pro)

Kling reads scene, subject, action, camera, audio in plain sentences.

**Pair 1, knees bent, back straight.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Workplace documentary, natural light. Tariq, a warehouse worker in his thirties with short dark hair, a navy work polo, grey work trousers, black safety boots and an orange high-visibility vest, stands upright facing a plain cardboard box on a concrete floor, feet shoulder-width apart. Medium shot from the side at hip height, 50mm look. Soft daylight from high skylights. Plain unlabelled shelving behind. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Workplace documentary, natural light, one continuous take. A warehouse aisle in soft skylight. Tariq bends his knees slowly into a squat beside the box, keeping his back straight and his head up, and places both hands on the sides of the box. Camera: locked-off side view, no movement. End state: Tariq in a squat, back straight, hands on the box. Tariq, the box and the aisle stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one person in frame. Sound: quiet warehouse ambience, the scuff of a boot. No music. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Pair 2, the grip.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Workplace documentary, natural light. Two hands in grey work gloves rest on the lower edges of a plain cardboard box on a concrete floor, the cuffs of a navy work polo and an orange high-visibility vest edge visible. Insert shot from the side, 85mm look, shallow depth of field. Soft daylight from above. Plain unlabelled box. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Workplace documentary, natural light, one continuous take. Insert shot. The hands slide under the base of the box, fingers spread, and grip it firmly; the box lifts two centimetres off the floor and holds steady. Camera: locked-off, no movement. End state: the box held just above the floor, level. The hands, the gloves and the box stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly two hands. Sound: cardboard creak. No music. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Second family, Veo 3.1 Fast.** The same text with the five-part order
(shot first) and sound labels: "Ambient noise: quiet warehouse hum. SFX:
cardboard creak." Use 4 s clips; durations 4 or 8 only.

## 9. Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Tariq's face or vest changes between steps | Character block verbatim; a reference image wired (Wan 2.7 takes 4) |
| The posture is wrong in the "right way" shot | One movement, slow, the end posture stated; re-roll once; or import real footage |
| Readable labels on boxes, shelves, signs | "plain unlabelled" in every still prompt |
| A second worker appears | "Exactly one person in frame" |
| Generic stock look (smiling team at a laptop) | A specific task with specific props |
| A safety step the policy does not contain | Remove it; ask the subject expert |

## 10. Policy guards

- Fictional employees, said to be a dramatisation in the module's
  description; no real employee's likeness or voice without written
  consent.
- Safety, legal and compliance content only from the user's documents,
  reviewed by their expert; never improvised.
- No AI person presented as a real colleague, manager or customer giving a
  statement.
- No real logos generated; the user adds branding in the app.
