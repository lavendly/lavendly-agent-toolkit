# Recipe: educational and concept explainer

Teach one concept (how, why, what is) so the viewer can explain it back.
Classrooms, course platforms, YouTube education, edu-TikTok. Worked
example: how a fridge keeps food cold. The user's own concept replaces it.

## 1. Purpose, audience, ending

- Purpose: one mental model built in the viewer's head, step by step, with
  a picture for every step.
- Audience: learners who chose to watch (students, the curious, course
  members). They forgive a slower pace and punish confusion.
- The ending states the concept in one repeatable sentence over the
  clearest picture of it, then asks one check-for-understanding question
  aloud ("So where does the heat from your fridge go?").

## 2. Learning rules that change the shot list

| Rule | In practice |
|---|---|
| One idea per scene | One sentence, one picture, one change in the picture |
| The picture shows the claim | "It moves heat out" shows heat leaving (shimmer above the back coil), not a fridge door |
| Pre-train key terms | Define the one term the film needs in the first third, with its picture |
| Picture and word at the same time | The picture of a step is on screen while its sentence is spoken; cut on the sentence |
| Cut what does not teach | No decorative b-roll, no music swells, no extra facts |
| Signal the important part | A slow push-in or a cut to a macro on the step that matters |
| Misconception first | Name the wrong idea the viewer already holds, then replace it |
| Concrete before abstract | The kitchen first, the thermodynamics second |
| Speak to the viewer | "your fridge", "you can feel it" |
| Check understanding | End on one question the viewer can answer from the film |

### 90 s

The 60 s plan plus a worked example (20 s: why a fridge's back is warm to
the touch, a hand near the coil, 2 shots) and an application (10 s: the
same idea runs an air conditioner and a heat pump, two matched shots of a
plain outdoor unit). About 200 words.

## 4. Hook and payoff

- Hook: the misconception, stated as fact and contradicted in the next
  line ("Your fridge does not make cold."). Or a question the viewer has
  had ("Why is the back of your fridge warm?").
- Payoff: the one-line model over its clearest picture ("A fridge is a heat
  mover, pointed inward."), static.
- Close on the check question; the answer is in the film.

## 5. Camera, light, colour

- Clean tabletop or domestic realism, one light recipe for the film:
  "soft night light from the fridge interior, the kitchen dark, a cool blue
  window behind" for the domestic shots; "one soft key from the left, white
  sweep background" for macro experiments.
- Moves: locked-off for every experiment (the change must read); slow
  push-in only to signal the key step; slow truck along a pipe or a line of
  objects.
- Colour: "cool whites and steel greys, one warm amber accent for heat".
  Keep heat warm and cold cool in every shot: colour is part of the lesson.
- Leave negative space for the user's labels: "the left third of the frame
  is plain dark wall".

## 6. Sound

- Narration 2.3 to 2.4 words per second; matilda, brian or alice.
- Native `mix` 0.3 (fridge hum, a pump's hiss, a drop sizzling); one clip
  `native` where the sound teaches (the compressor starting), with a gap
  in the script.
- Score: "Light, clear and curious: muted piano and soft pizzicato strings
  at about 90 BPM. Opens on two notes under the misconception, adds a
  steady pulse through the mechanism, resolves on a warm major chord under
  the takeaway, silent for the final question." Volume 0.25, ducking on.
- Captions: `youtube` on 16:9, `reels` on 9:16, `caption` (boxed) for a
  course or classroom.

## 8. Prompt pairs (Veo 3.1 Fast)

**Pair 1, scene 1, the misconception.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Clean domestic film, soft night light. A plain white fridge with no logo stands closed in a dark kitchen at night, a cool blue window behind it, a tiled floor in front. Medium shot at eye level, 35mm look, moderate depth of field. The only light is the blue window and a thin line of light at the fridge door seal. Matte white enamel, chrome handle. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Clean domestic film, soft night light, one continuous take. Medium shot, eye level. 0 to 1 s: the kitchen is still. 1 to 3 s: the fridge door swings open toward the camera on its own, slowly, and cool white light spills across the floor tiles. 3 to 4 s: a faint cold mist rolls out over the bottom shelf and sinks. Camera: locked-off, no movement. End state: door open, the floor lit in a pale rectangle. The fridge, the kitchen and the window stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one fridge, no person in frame. SFX: a soft door seal release, the fridge hum rising. No music. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Pair 2, scene 3, inside the pipes.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Macro science film, clean studio light. A single bare copper pipe runs across the frame from left to right against a dark grey background, its surface dry and warm orange-brown. Macro shot from the side, 100mm macro look, focus plane millimetres deep along the pipe. One soft key light from the left, gentle falloff into shadow on the right. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Macro science film, clean studio light, one continuous take. Macro, side view. 0 to 1 s: the pipe is dry. 1 to 4 s: white frost crystals form on the left end of the pipe and spread steadily to the right, feathered and fine, covering half the pipe by the end. Camera: slow truck left to right along the pipe at the speed of the frost, stopping at the frost line. End state: the left half white with frost, the right half bare copper. The pipe, the background and the light stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one pipe. SFX: a faint crackle of ice forming. No music. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

## 9. Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| A labelled cutaway diagram of a fridge, letters garbled | Replace with physical experiments (drop on steel, frost on copper, warm pump nozzle) |
| A brand logo on the appliance | "a plain white fridge with no logo"; frame the handle side |
| The experiment does not happen (no frost forms) | One change only, slow, with start and end states and a destination ("covering half the pipe") |
| Colours of heat and cold swap between shots | Palette anchors in every still prompt: heat warm amber, cold blue-white |
| Narration explains while the picture shows something else | Re-time: cut on the sentence; every picture appears with its own sentence |
| Too many ideas in 30 s | One concept; anything else becomes the next film |

## 10. Policy guards

- Analogies are declared as analogies. A physical demonstration stands in
  for a mechanism; it is never presented as footage of the inside of the
  real machine.
- Safety: never show a viewer an unsafe experiment to copy (open gas, live
  wiring, chemicals mixed); describe it or choose a safe one.
- Facts from settled science or the user's source; no invented figures.
