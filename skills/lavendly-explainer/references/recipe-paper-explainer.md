# Recipe: paper explainer

One research paper, under a minute, drawn like the figures in a patent: ink
line art with hatched shading on cream paper, one accent colour for the
thing being talked about, short labels with leader lines, dotted
construction lines, a stamp when the status matters. The viewer leaves
knowing the question, the trick, the number and the catch. Worked example
throughout: a preprint on a robot arm that fits a peg into a tight hole by
feel alone, no camera and no touch sensor. The user's paper replaces it.

Golden rule: one claim per drawing, and every claim wears its origin (in
simulation, on the real robot, the authors' own caveat).

## 1. Purpose, audience, ending

- Purpose: make one paper repeatable in a sentence by someone who will
  never read it, without overselling it. Research channels, lab accounts,
  newsletters turned into video, a founder explaining the paper behind a
  product.
- Audience: curious technical viewers scrolling; they trust the film when
  it says what was not tested as plainly as what was.
- The ending: the limits said out loud, the publication status said out
  loud, one question back to the viewer, and the link promised in the
  caption. Never "this changes everything".

## 2. The nine beats

| # | Beat | What it does | Drawing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The question | The task as a question the viewer can picture | The machine and the task, the usual tools labelled |
| 2 | The twist | What the paper removes or adds | The same drawing, the removed tools struck through in the accent |
| 3 | Who tried it | The lab, the paper, in one sentence | A paper card, blank, the title drawn in post |
| 4 | The trick | The idea in one physical picture | The part of the machine that carries the idea, ringed in the accent |
| 5 | The method | How they trained or tested it, with its origin tag | The grid, the many cases, a boxed sim tag |
| 6 | The transfer | What moved from the test bench to the world | The same machine drawn solid, a boxed real tag |
| 7 | The number | One headline result, spoken and captioned | A field of small outlines, some filled, no digits |
| 8 | The comparison | Against the obvious alternative, with its origin tag | Two hatched bars, no axis values |
| 9 | Limits, status, ask | What it does not do, preprint or reviewed, a question | The ghosted untested cases; the card with a stamp; the opening drawing again |

A 30 s film merges beats 1 and 2, and 5 and 6. A 60 s film gives each beat
one or two shots. Beat 9 is never cut: a paper explainer without its limits
is an advert.

## 3. What Lavendly cannot do, and what to do instead

| The reference film | On Lavendly |
|---|---|
| Motion graphics with exact lettering: titles, author names, the identifier, big serif numerals | The picture carries at most three short labels, nouns the viewer has already heard, checked in the frame; the title, the authors, the identifier and every number live in the narration, the captions and two cards the user draws in the app over a blank still |
| A word-by-word caption highlight in the accent colour | Captions burn in chunks of three words, no per-word highlight; the accent belongs to the drawing. Pick `reels` on 9:16 or 1:1, `youtube` on 16:9, `caption` when the text must carry alone |
| Charts with axes and values | A field of outlines with some filled, two hatched bars of different height: direction and rough size only; the value is spoken and captioned, with "about" |
| A 4:5 frame | 9:16 for Shorts and Reels or 1:1 for feeds, the drawing in the middle band with cream above and below |
| One continuous animation | One clip per shot, one named scene per shot in film order, each a drawing that holds while one element moves |
| The paper's own figure | A redrawing in this look, never a copy of the figure; the narration says "redrawn" when the user asks for the figure |

## 4. Structures by length

Word budget 2.4 words per second (the server's ceiling is 2.5): 30 s is 72
words, 60 s is 140. Numbers spelled out as spoken, one per sentence.

second; on Lavendly that script is cut to the budget, never read faster.

### 90 s

Add a "how we know" chapter after the number (the test rig drawn, the
count of trials said, the source of the number named) and a second limit
or the authors' own next step. About 210 words.

## 5. Visual language

- Style line, first in every prompt: "Technical illustration on cream
  paper, black ink line art with hatched shading, flat, no gradients".
- One accent colour for the whole film, named once ("signal orange"), used
  only for the thing the sentence is about: a ring, a strike-through, a
  path, a filled outline, a stamp.
- Drawing grammar: a ground line under every machine; dotted construction
  lines for paths and planned positions; hatching for solid parts; a thin
  leader line from each label to its part; a boxed one-word tag (sim or
  real) in a corner when the origin matters.
- Labels: at most three per still, one or two words each, lower case,
  nouns the narration has already said. The image model letters them; the
  frame is checked before the clip, and a misspelt label is re-rolled or
  removed from the prompt. Never a number, a name, a title, an equation or
  a sentence in the picture.
- Blank where post text goes: the paper card is "a blank sheet with faint
  grey lines too soft to read"; the summary frame is cream with one ground
  line and nothing else.
- No people. A hand, when needed, is line art and enters from the edge.
- The same machine, drawn the same way in every shot: one locked
  description (parts, proportions, the hatch direction) pasted verbatim.

## 6. Motion: the drawing holds, one thing moves

- Camera: locked-off in every shot. The look reads as a page; a camera
  move breaks it.
- One element moves per clip, over 1 to 2 s, then holds: the peg lowers
  into the hole; a dotted path draws itself; a hatch fill creeps up the
  outlines; an accent ring pulses once; a strike-through draws across a
  label; a stamp lands and settles; outlines slide in from the right and
  stop. Everything else stays exactly as in the opening frame.
- Hard cuts on sentence ends. No dissolves, no wipes. Beat changes may
  take a 2 s hold on the last drawing.
- The number shot: the outlines fill in order, left to right, top to
  bottom, and stop short of the end; the exact share is in the caption.

## 7. Narration and captions

- Script first, inside the budget. Sentence shapes: a question, then the
  answer in the next sentence; "The trick:" before the idea; "So" before
  the method; "But" before the limits; one question at the end.
- Every claim carries its origin: "in simulation", "on the real robot",
  "the authors say". A result from simulation is never read as a result
  in the world.
- Numbers: spelled out, one per sentence, "about" when rounded, compared
  to the drawing ("about three in four").
- The status is said in words: "a preprint, not peer-reviewed yet", or
  "published in a reviewed journal", from the user's source.
- Captions on: they carry the numbers. Chunks of three words, so the
  sentence is written to break well ("seventy-three percent" stays
  together).
- Two cards drawn in the app after the render: the paper card at beat 3
  (title, authors, venue or identifier) and the summary card at the end
  (the number, the limits, the status), both on the blank stills left for
  them. The link goes in the post caption, as the narration promises.

## 8. Sound

- Narration leads: one voice, dry and quick, one track with the whole
  script, `"scope": "film"`, confirmed with `get_workflow_audio`.
- Clips `off`: drawings make no sound. A pencil tick on a strike-through
  or a soft thud on the stamp only if the user wants it, then the clip
  stays `native` at `mix` 0.2.
- One score: "Minimal and precise: a felt piano and a soft ticking pulse
  at about ninety BPM, no melody, a single low note under the limits, out
  before the last card." Volume 0.2, `ducking: true`, `"scope": "film"`.

## 9. Models and credits

| Pick | Model | Why | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Default | Veo 3.1 Fast | Holds a flat drawing still and moves one element cleanly; 4 s clips | about 132 | about 245 |
| Long holds, 16:9 | Hailuo 2.3 | 10 s for 20 credits where a drawing holds under two sentences | about 70 | about 130 |
| Draft | Veo 3.1 Lite (Auto) | The cheapest pass to check the look and the labels | about 100 | about 190 |

Opening images about 2 credits each from the default image model; when a
label misspells twice, ask the user to draw that still in the app with the
image model that letters best and place it by `list_assets`. Totals
include one voiceover and one score. Durations come from each model's
exact list (Veo 4, 6, 8 only).

## 10. Prompt pairs (Veo 3.1 Fast)

**Pair 1, the question.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Technical illustration on cream paper, black ink line art with hatched shading, flat, no gradients. A six-joint robot arm drawn from the side holds a short cylindrical peg above a block with one tight slot, on a hatched ground line. Two small lower-case labels with thin leader lines: camera at the wrist, touch at the gripper. Centred, the drawing in the middle band, cream space above and below. No other text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Technical illustration on cream paper, black ink line art with hatched shading, flat, no gradients, one continuous take. Side view of the robot arm above the slot. 0 to 2 s: a thin signal orange line draws itself across the label camera, then across the label touch, left to right. 2 to 4 s: everything holds still. Camera: locked-off, no movement. End state: both labels struck through in orange, the arm unmoved. The arm, the block, the labels and the hatching stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one arm, one peg, one block. No music. No new text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Pair 2, the number.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Technical illustration on cream paper, black ink line art with hatched shading, flat, no gradients. A grid of one hundred small identical peg outlines, ten by ten, all empty, above a hatched ground line, centred in the middle band with cream space above and below. No labels, no numbers, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Technical illustration on cream paper, black ink line art with hatched shading, flat, no gradients, one continuous take. The ten by ten grid of peg outlines. 0 to 3 s: the outlines fill with solid black hatching one by one, left to right and top to bottom, and the filling stops after about three quarters of the grid. 3 to 4 s: hold. Camera: locked-off, no movement. End state: about three quarters filled, the rest empty outlines. The grid, the ground line and the paper stay exactly as in the opening frame. No music. No text, no numbers, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Second family, Seedance (production note).**

```text
STARTING STATE: a side-view ink drawing of a robot arm holding a peg above a tight slot on cream paper, hatched shading, two lower-case labels on leader lines. TIMELINE: [0-2s] a signal orange line strikes through each label in turn. [2-4s] hold. CAMERA: locked-off. AUDIO: none, no music. ENDING STATE: the same drawing with both labels struck through, held.
```

## 11. Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| A label misspelt or a word invented in the still | Fewer labels (one), or none; re-roll the still; the word goes to the captions |
| The video model re-letters a label as it moves | Keep labels away from the moving element; "the labels stay exactly as in the opening frame"; or move nothing near them |
| Digits or a percentage appear | Remove "chart", "percent", "data" from the prompt; outlines and bars only, "no numbers" last |
| The drawing turns photoreal or shaded with gradients | Restate the style line first; "flat, no gradients, ink on paper"; drop "3D", "render", "realistic" |
| The arm changes shape between shots | One locked description pasted verbatim; a reference image of the first still on a model that takes references |
| The whole page pans or zooms | "Camera: locked-off, no movement" and nothing about "reveal" or "push" |
| The script runs over the picture | Cut words, never speed the voice; the server freezes the last frame when the voice is longer |
| A simulation result reads as a real one | Add the origin to the sentence and the sim tag to the drawing |
| A photoreal hand with a marker enters while a line or ring "draws itself" | Say the element "fades in" or "appears" instead of "draws"; add "no hand, no pen, no marker, no person"; when it still appears, keep the clean stretch of the take and hold its last frame (the renderer holds a clip on its last frame to fill its box) |
| The page curls, shows a notebook edge or a desk, or turns into a photograph | The opening frame says the paper "fills the entire frame edge to edge, no page edges, no notebook, no desk"; a still that already shows an edge is redrawn, never re-rolled |
| The captions skip a word the voice says | Read the voice track's words with `get_workflow_audio` before the first render; a dropped word is put back with its timing through `update_workflow` on the voice node, then render again (free) |

## 12. Policy guards

- The title, the authors, the venue, the identifier and every number come
  from the user's source (the paper, its page, its abstract). Nothing is
  completed from memory; a missing number is asked for, not guessed.
- The publication status is stated as the source gives it. A preprint is
  called a preprint; "not peer-reviewed yet" is said when that is true.
- The limits section repeats what the authors themselves report as not
  done or not tested; the film never implies a capability the paper
  withholds.
- A redrawn figure is called redrawn; the film never presents an
  illustration as the paper's data.
- Institutions and people are named in the narration and the cards only,
  never drawn as logos or likenesses.
- A paper on health, finance or safety stays general information; "talk
  to a professional" when the audience could act on it.

## 13. A finished film: the drone race

The showcase film for this recipe, made in Lavendly with Veo 3.1 Fast:
https://lavendly.ai/showcase/paper-explainer.mp4 (32 s, 9:16). The paper
is the vision-only racing drone that beat three champion pilots
(Kaufmann and others, Nature, 2023); every fact below is from the paper.
Eight shots of 4 s, 74 words, captions `reels`, clips `off`, score at 0.2
with ducking, two cards left blank for post (beats 3 and 9).

| # | Beat | Picture | Moving element |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Question | The quadrotor before a square gate, labels camera and gate | An orange ring around the camera lens |
| 2 | Who | A blank sheet on a desk (title card in post) | Nothing; the sheet holds |
| 3 | Trick | The quadrotor among dotted gates on a grid, boxed sim tag | An orange circle draws the lap |
| 4 | Transfer | The quadrotor drawn solid before a real gate, boxed real tag | An orange flown path beside the dotted plan |
| 5 | Number | Twenty-five small square outlines, five by five | Fifteen fill orange, in order, then stop |
| 6 | Comparison | Two hatched bars, labels drone and pilot, no values | An orange ring around the shorter bar's end |
| 7 | Limits | The quadrotor on the ground below the gate, a second gate ghosted | The drone settles; nothing else moves |
| 8 | Status and ask | A sketched paper sheet, cream space below (summary card in post) | An orange stamp ring lands |

Script: "Can a drone beat champion pilots using only its own camera? A
Zurich robotics lab built one. The trick: it learned to race entirely in
simulation. Then about fifty seconds of real flight corrected the
simulator. Against three champions, it won fifteen of twenty-five races.
Its fastest lap beat the best human by half a second. But one crash ends
its race, and it knows one track. Published in Nature. Link in the
caption."

What it cost to get clean: three of the eight takes grew a photoreal hand
with a marker, one blank sheet curled and gained hands, and the transcript
dropped four words; the fixes are the last three rows of section 11.
