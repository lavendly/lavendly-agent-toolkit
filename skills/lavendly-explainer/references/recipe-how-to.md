# Recipe: how-to and tutorial

Steps toward a result the viewer can reproduce. Searchers, learners,
customers of a product. Worked example: repotting a houseplant. Software
tutorials are covered in section 9.

## 1. Purpose, audience, ending

- Purpose: the viewer can do it after one viewing, in the right order,
  without a step skipped.
- Audience: people with the task in front of them; they pause, rewind and
  copy. Clarity beats pace.
- Open on the finished result; end on the same result and a one-line recap.
  Never a hook-and-payoff trick; the result is the promise.

## 2. Rules that change the shot list

- Steps in order, one step per clip, one action per step. A step with two
  actions is two clips.
- Every step shows its result state before the next step starts (the root
  ball out of the old pot, then the new pot filled a third with soil).
- Hands and tools: the contact point is centred and lit; both hands visible
  when both work; one locked angle per step, top-down or over the
  shoulder, kept the same across steps so the viewer's eye learns where to
  look.
- Continuity is strict: the plant, the pot, the soil level, the tool in
  hand carry from step to step (the ledger in lavendly-director). Chain the
  next step's opening frame from the last frame of the previous one when
  the action continues; after two or three hops re-anchor with a clean
  still from the reference.
- Precise manual steps (a knot, a stitch, a cut, an assembly) are where
  models fail: show the start state, a simple action and the result; import
  the user's real footage for the exact movement.
- Step numbers and measurements are spoken ("Step three. Fill the pot a
  third of the way."); the user adds on-screen numbers in the app.

### 90 s

Add a "what not to do" mini-sequence (packing the soil, a pot with no
drainage hole, three shots of 4 s) and a "one week later" shot (a new leaf
unfurling, a time-passage hold of 8 s). About 195 words.

## 4. Hook and payoff

- Hook: the finished result plus the time it takes ("A happy plant in five
  minutes.").
- Payoff: the same result in the same framing at the end, so the viewer
  sees they arrived; a one-line recap of the steps in order.

## 5. Camera, light, colour

- "Top-down, camera pointing straight down over a pale wooden potting
  bench" for steps; "over the shoulder, from behind the right shoulder" for
  steps where depth matters; "medium close-up, three-quarter" for the
  result.
- Light: "bright even soft daylight from a large window on the left, no
  hard shadows on the work area".
- Colour: "true-to-life colours, warm wood, terracotta, deep green leaves";
  accurate colour matters when the viewer must match it.
- Every step clip locked-off; the only moves are the result push-in and a
  tilt that reveals a sign.

## 6. Sound

- Narration imperative and numbered at 2.3 words per second; will, sarah
  or chris.
- Native `mix` 0.4: soil rustle, pot scrape, water pour are useful cues.
- Score: "Warm, easy acoustic guitar and soft brushes at about 95 BPM.
  Opens with a single strum on the result, keeps a light steady pattern
  through the steps, ends on one open chord on the final result."
  Volume 0.25, ducking on.
- Captions: `reels` or `tiktok` on 9:16, `youtube` on 16:9, `caption` for
  a course.

## 8. Prompt pairs (Veo 3.1 Fast)

Hands block, pasted in every still prompt: "two hands with short clean
nails and rolled grey linen sleeves".

**Pair 1, step: the root ball into the new pot.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Clean studio tabletop film, soft even light. Two hands with short clean nails and rolled grey linen sleeves hold a small monstera with its root ball above an empty terracotta pot, a third filled with dark soil, on a pale wooden bench. Top-down, camera pointing straight down, 35mm look, deep focus. Bright soft daylight from a window on the left, no hard shadows. A blank kraft bag of soil at the top edge. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Clean studio tabletop film, soft even light, one continuous take. Top-down. 0 to 2 s: the hands lower the root ball straight down into the centre of the pot. 2 to 4 s: the hands let go and press the soil around it twice with the fingertips, the top of the root ball sitting just below the rim. Camera: locked-off, no movement. End state: the plant upright in the centre of the pot, hands lifting away. The plant, the pot, the bench and the light stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly two hands, one plant, one pot, no other person. SFX: soil rustle, a soft scrape of terracotta. No music. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Pair 2, the result.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Clean studio tabletop film, soft even light. A monstera with five deep green split leaves stands upright in a terracotta pot of dark, evenly levelled soil on a pale wooden bench. Medium close-up, three-quarter angle, 50mm look, shallow depth of field. Bright soft daylight from a window on the left. Warm wood, terracotta, deep green. The right third of the frame is plain cream wall. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Clean studio tabletop film, soft even light, one continuous take. Medium close-up. 0 to 4 s: the leaves move very slightly in a soft draught; nothing else changes. Camera: slow push-in until the pot fills the lower half of the frame, then holds. End state: the plant centred, still. The plant, the pot and the light stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one plant, no hands, no person. Ambient noise: quiet room tone. No music. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Second family, Wan 2.7, with references.**

Wire the plant reference and the pot reference; say each one's job: "Reference 1 is the monstera's leaf shape and colour only. Reference 2 is the terracotta pot's shape and colour only; ignore both backgrounds." Motion text: "The hands lower the root ball into the centre of the pot and press the soil twice. Static shot, top-down. Even soft daylight, true colour. Exactly two hands. Sound: soil rustle. No music."

## 9. Software and app tutorials

- Never generate an interface. The model invents buttons, misspells
  labels and changes layouts between shots.
- The user records the real screen; `import_asset` (kind video, 3 minutes
  and 64 MB at most by url; bigger files through the Asset Library upload) and place it as a ready clip
  (mcp-calls.md). Trim each step with `edit_clip`; a digital punch-in with
  `crop` no tighter than about 1.2 times, or the text softens.
- Generated shots carry the human context only: hands on a keyboard with
  the screen turned away, a person at a desk seen from behind, a coffee cup
  by a closed laptop.
- Narration names the exact menu words the user gave ("Open Settings, then
  Privacy"); captions carry them.

## 10. Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Soil or tools appear from nowhere | Name every object in the still prompt and where it sits; containers say what they hold |
| The pot changes shape or colour between steps | The pot reference wired (Wan 2.7, Seedance) and the pot words pasted verbatim |
| A step's result is undone in the next shot | Chain the next opening frame from the previous last frame; update the ledger from the landed frame |
| Hands with extra fingers or a wrong grip | One action, slow, the contact point named; frame the hands at medium close-up, not macro |
| The task done wrong (a knot that is not a bowline) | Show start and result only; import real footage for the exact motion |
| Labels readable on bags or tools | "a blank kraft bag", "a plain steel trowel" |
| Steps blur together | One locked angle per step; a cut on each step's sentence |

## 11. Policy guards

- Safety-critical steps (electricity, gas, chemicals, ladders, food
  safety) come from the user's source or manufacturer instructions; show
  the protective step on screen (gloves on, power off) and say it.
- A product tutorial never shows an AI-generated result as what the real
  product achieves; real results come from the user's footage.
- No real brand's packaging or logo generated.
