# Recipe: history

A past event, era, invention or idea, told so the viewer understands what
changed and why it matters now. Worked example: how the printing press
changed the spread of news after the fourteen-fifties.

## 1. Purpose, audience, ending

- Purpose: a clear before, a cause, a sequence and an after, with the
  period made tangible through objects, materials and light.
- Audience: history channels, classrooms, museum and heritage pages. They
  notice anachronisms.
- The ending connects the change to something the viewer knows today
  ("Every newspaper since is a copy of a copy of that idea."), over a
  quiet object shot.

## 2. Rules that shape the film

- No real person's likeness, living or historical. Name people only in the
  narration; never put a real name in a prompt. Show what they made and
  touched: the press, hands setting type, a workshop, a figure from behind
  at a bench, a silhouette in a doorway.
- Reconstruction, never fake archive. Generated scenes are presented as
  reconstructions. For events after about 1890, when real film and
  photographs exist, never use an aged-film look that could pass for real
  archive; use a painterly, diorama or clean reconstruction look instead,
  and import real archive material the user has rights to.
- Period accuracy is written into every still prompt: materials, light
  sources, clothing fabrics and colours, tools (table in section 9).
- Dates and numbers come from the user's sources or settled consensus and
  are spoken ("in fourteen fifty"); contested points are attributed
  ("historians estimate").
- Maps are unlabelled relief or a thread or ink line across a plain
  surface; place names are spoken.
- War, persecution and disaster: an illustrated look (paper, clay,
  painterly still life), objects and places rather than suffering bodies,
  no gore, no reconstruction of real victims.

### 90 s

Add the consequence chapter (25 s: cheaper books, more readers, arguments
spreading faster than rulers liked; four shots: a schoolroom slate, a
candle-lit reader, a stack of pamphlets burned in a brazier, a printer's
shop shutter closing) and a dated step sequence. About 200 words.

## 4. Hook and payoff

- Hook: a then-and-now contrast or the moment of change ("Before this
  press, books were copied by hand.") over the key object.
- Payoff: the consequence that reaches the viewer's life, over a present
  day object matched in framing to the opening object (a match cut).

## 5. Camera, light, colour

- Style line, one per film: "Period reconstruction, natural and candle
  light, muted colour" or "Painterly still life, oil paint texture, single
  window light" or "Handmade paper diorama".
- Moves: slow truck across objects, slow push-in on the turning point, slow
  pull-out on the takeaway; timeline beats pan in one direction.
- Light by period: candle and hearth, oil lamp, north window, later gas
  and tungsten. Name it in every still prompt.
- Colour: "umber, ochre, soot black, linen white" for early modern;
  "sepia browns, faded greens" for the nineteenth century; never a teal and
  orange grade.

## 6. Sound

- Narration past tense, 2.3 words per second; george, daniel or alice.
- Native `mix` 0.3 (the press creak, a quill scratch, a crowd murmur at
  distance); "No music." in every motion prompt.
- Score: "Restrained early-music colour: a viol and a plucked lute line at
  about 70 BPM, opening alone under the hand-copying, adding a low drum
  pulse as the press starts, widening on the spread, settling on a single
  sustained note under the present-day shot." Volume 0.25, ducking on.
- Captions: `youtube` on 16:9.

## 8. Prompt pairs (Veo 3.1 Fast)

**Pair 1, the type.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Period reconstruction, natural and candle light, muted colour. A wooden composing stick holds a row of small cast metal type pieces, their faces too small and worn to read, on a dark oak bench beside a tray of loose type. Macro shot, 100mm macro look, focus plane on the front edge. One candle flame from the left, warm, deep shadows on the right. Umber, soot black, brass. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Period reconstruction, natural and candle light, muted colour, one continuous take. Macro. 0 to 2 s: the candle light flickers softly over the metal. 2 to 4 s: two fingertips place one more piece of type at the end of the row and press it flush. Camera: slow truck right along the row, stopping on the new piece. End state: the row one piece longer, the fingers lifting away. The stick, the bench and the candle light stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly two fingertips, no face. SFX: a small metallic click. No music. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Pair 2, the press.**

Still-frame prompt:

```text
Period reconstruction, natural and candle light, muted colour. A wooden hand press with a large iron screw and a long bar stands in a stone-walled workshop, a sheet of blank paper on the bed, ink balls on a stool. Medium shot at chest height, 35mm look, moderate depth of field. Cool daylight from a high window on the left, warm candle light on the right. Oak, iron, linen white. Clean frame, no text.
```

Motion prompt:

```text
Period reconstruction, natural and candle light, muted colour, one continuous take. Medium shot. 0 to 2 s: a printer in a linen shirt, seen from behind, pulls the long bar toward himself. 2 to 4 s: the screw turns and the platen presses down on the paper, then the bar swings back. Camera: locked-off, no movement. End state: the bar back in place, the paper flat on the bed. The press, the workshop and the light stay exactly as in the opening frame. Exactly one person, seen from behind only, face never visible. SFX: wood creak, a heavy thud. Ambient noise: a quiet room, distant street voices. No music. Clean frame: no text, no subtitles, no on-screen graphics.
```

**Second family, Hailuo 2.3 (silent, bracket camera command).**

```text
A row of cast metal type in a wooden stick on an oak bench, one candle from the left. Two fingertips place one more piece at the end of the row and press it flush [Truck right]. Warm candle light, deep shadows, muted colour.
```

## 9. Period accuracy

| Period | Light | Materials and objects | Clothing | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ancient Mediterranean | Oil lamps, sun, braziers | Clay, bronze, stone, papyrus, wax tablets | Wool and linen tunics, cloaks, sandals | Paper books, stirrups (early), glass windows in homes |
| Medieval Europe | Candles, rush lights, hearth, small windows | Wood, wrought iron, parchment, pewter | Wool, linen, dyed in earth tones; hoods | Printed books before 1450, potatoes, tomatoes |
| 1450 to 1600 | Candles, north windows | Oak, iron, linen paper, brass | Doublets, linen shirts, wool hose | Buttons used everywhere, zips, cotton in Europe at scale |
| 1600 to 1800 | Candles, oil lamps, larger glazed windows | Porcelain, pewter, quill pens | Waistcoats, breeches, stays, wool and silk | Electric light, matches (before 1820s), steel nibs |
| 1800 to 1880 | Oil and gas lamps | Cast iron, steam, glass bottles, early photography | Frock coats, crinolines | Electric bulbs, telephones, plastics |
| 1880 to 1940 | Tungsten bulbs arrive, gas in older homes | Bakelite later, early cars, typewriters | Suits, hats on most adults, cotton dresses | Wristwatches before 1910 on most men, nylon before 1940 |
| 1940 to 1990 | Tungsten and fluorescent | Plastics, televisions, rotary then push-button phones | Era-specific; ask the user for the decade | Mobile phones before the 1980s, flat screens |

Write the era's light and two or three objects into every still prompt.

## 10. Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| A face that resembles a famous figure | Remove names; show from behind, hands, objects |
| Readable but wrong letters on pages and signs | "ink lines too soft to read", "a blank shop sign board" |
| Anachronisms (electric light, zips, modern glass) | The period row of section 9 in the still prompt |
| A map with invented place names | "an unlabelled relief map in one colour"; places spoken |
| Aged film look that passes for archive | Choose the painterly, diorama or clean reconstruction look |
| Costume drifts between scenes | One clothing line per character type, pasted verbatim |

## 11. Policy guards

- Reconstruction is said: in the narration where natural ("a
  reconstruction of a workshop like his") or in the post caption.
- No real person's likeness, no real person's name in prompts.
- No fake archive for events with real film or photographs.
- Sensitive history in an illustrated look, without gore or real victims.
- Dates, numbers and quotes from sources; no invented quotes put in a
  historical person's mouth.
